Reporting verbs don't all behave the same way. Some are followed by "that",
some are followed by the "-ing" form, and some are followed by "to" plus
the infinitive. Let's look at all three.

First: verbs followed by "that". These are the verbs we use when we're
simply reporting a fact or a statement. For example: "She admitted that
she was late." "He explained that the museum was closed." Other common
verbs in this group are: agree, announce, argue, claim, complain, deny,
insist, mention, promise, recommend, reply, say, suggest, and warn.

Second: verbs followed by the "-ing" form. Notice that many of these verbs
have a hidden preposition just before the "-ing". For example: "He apologised
FOR being late." "She insisted ON paying." "They accused him OF lying."
Other verbs in this group include: admit to, deny, mention, propose,
recommend, and suggest.

Third: verbs followed by "to" plus the infinitive. We use these when we're
talking about promises, plans, offers, or orders — things that will happen
in the future. For example: "He promised to call." "She agreed to help."
"They ordered him to leave." Other verbs in this group are: advise, arrange,
ask, decide, demand, encourage, forbid, invite, offer, persuade, plan,
refuse, remind, tell, threaten, urge, and warn.

Here's a simple way to remember it: a FACT takes "that". A hidden
PREPOSITION takes "-ing". And the FUTURE takes "to".

Fact — that. Preposition — ing. Future — to.

Now try making your own sentences with these verbs, and check which
group each one belongs to.

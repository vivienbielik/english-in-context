AUDIO SCRIPT 2 — for the "Listening Extracts" section, new topic:
"Reported Verbs in Context"
(Target length: ~90 seconds when read at natural pace, natural storytelling voice)

---

Last week, our school organised a small exhibition of student paintings.
I want to tell you what happened, because a lot of interesting things
were said.

The art teacher announced that the exhibition would open on Friday
afternoon. She explained that every student could show one piece of work.
One boy, Peter, admitted that he was nervous about showing his painting
in public. His friend Lucia agreed to help him hang it on the wall.

During the opening, a visitor approached one of the paintings and asked
who had made it. Peter's classmate proudly said that it was Peter's work.
The visitor praised the painting and suggested entering it in a regional
art competition. Peter promised to think about it.

Later, one teacher accused a student of copying a famous painting style —
but the student explained that it was intentional, a kind of tribute. The
teacher apologised for jumping to conclusions, and everyone laughed about
it afterwards.

At the end of the day, the head teacher thanked all the students and
invited them to organise another exhibition next term. Most students
agreed to take part again.

Now think back through the story. Can you remember which reporting verb
was used for each situation — and whether it was followed by "that",
by "-ing", or by "to"?

2Learn

The Baroque period in music lasted from about 1600 to 1750. Music from this time is often grand, dramatic, and full of energy. Composers used a small keyboard instrument called the harpsichord, and they liked strong contrasts — loud and quiet, fast and slow.

🎹 Who was Handel?

George Frideric Handel (1685–1759) was born in Germany but spent most of his life in London, England. He wrote operas, orchestral music (like "Water Music"), and religious works called oratorios.

📖 What is an oratorio?

An oratorio is like an opera, but without costumes, acting, or scenery. Singers and an orchestra tell a story — often a religious story — just through music.

The story of Messiah

Summer 1741 — A friend gave Handel a text about the life of Jesus Christ. Handel started writing music very quickly.
24 days — Handel wrote the whole oratorio — about 260 pages of music — in only 24 days! He worked so hard that he almost forgot to eat.
13 April 1742 — "Messiah" had its first performance in Dublin, Ireland. Over 700 people came to listen, and the money helped free 142 people from prison.
Today — "Messiah" is performed every year all around the world, especially at Christmas and Easter.

🎉 The famous "Hallelujah Chorus"

The most famous part of "Messiah" is the Hallelujah Chorus. There is a tradition that the audience stands up during this chorus — people say it started because King George II stood up the first time he heard it in London!

4Listen

🎵 Extract 1 — "Hallelujah Chorus" from Messiah (Handel)
Listen and think:
  1. How many times can you count the word "Hallelujah"?
  2. Does the music feel happy, sad, or triumphant (very joyful and proud)?
  3. Can you hear the chorus (many singers) and the orchestra playing together?
🎵 Extract 2 — "Alla Hornpipe" from Water Music (Handel)
Listen and think:
  1. This music has no singing. What instruments can you hear?
  2. Is the rhythm dance-like? Could you march or walk to it?
  3. Handel wrote this for a royal party on a boat! Can you imagine why it sounds so grand?

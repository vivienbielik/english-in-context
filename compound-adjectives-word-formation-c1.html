Two Words, One Meaning

Compound adjectives such as level-headed, narrow-minded, well-known and time-consuming join two words into one precise description. Learn the eight patterns, the hyphen rules and the most useful personality adjectives, then test yourself in six exercises.

What is a compound adjective?

A compound adjective is made of two (or more) words that work together as one adjective. Usually they are joined with a hyphen.

One idea, fewer words

a person who keeps calm in difficult situations → a level-headed person

The hyphen avoids confusion

a man-eating shark (a shark that eats people) vs. a man eating shark (a man who is eating shark!)

Why it matters at C1

Compound adjectives are typical in Maturita word-formation tasks and make your speaking and writing sound precise and natural.

The 8 main patterns

Each pattern follows a clear rule. Learn the rule and you can understand, and often create, new compounds.

A

Adjective / number + noun + -ed

narrow + mind + -ed → narrow-minded

Rule: describes a quality or a physical feature a person has. We add -ed to the noun, even though it looks like a past participle. This is the most important pattern for describing personality.

level-headednarrow-mindedopen-mindedkind-heartedcold-bloodedshort-temperedthick-skinnedbig-headedstrong-willedquick-wittedabsent-mindedtwo-facedblue-eyedleft-handedbroad-shouldered
  • She has a cool head in a crisis. → She is level-headed.
  • He is unwilling to accept other opinions. → He is narrow-minded.
  • a girl with long legs → a long-legged girl
💡 Watch out: the noun stays singular: a blue-eyed boy (not blue-eyesed), a two-faced politician.
B

Noun + noun + -ed

pig + head + -ed → pig-headed

Rule: the first noun works like a comparison ("like a…"). The meaning is often metaphorical.

pig-headed (very stubborn)lion-hearted (very brave)eagle-eyed (noticing every detail)chicken-hearted (cowardly)
  • An eagle-eyed student spotted the mistake in the test.
C

Adverb + past participle

well + known → well-known

Rule: passive meaning: something is done to the person or thing. Very common first words: well, badly, ill, highly, much, newly, widely.

well-knownwell-behavedwell-educatedwell-off (rich)well-informedill-informedill-equippedmuch-lovednewly-builthighly respectedbadly paid
  • Her opinions are always based on good information. → She is well-informed.
  • a scientist who is respected by many people → a highly respected scientist
💡 Adverbs ending in -ly (highly, badly, newly) are normally written without a hyphen: a highly respected teacher. See Rule 3 below.
D

Adjective / adverb + present participle (-ing)

easy + going → easy-going

Rule: active meaning: the person or thing does something or is a certain way all the time.

easy-goinghard-workinggood-lookinglong-lastingfar-reachingnever-endingslow-movingfree-thinking
  • The new law will have far-reaching consequences (= affecting many things).
  • a friendship that lasts for a long time → a long-lasting friendship
E

Noun + present participle (-ing)

time + consuming → time-consuming

Rule: the noun is the object of the verb: it consumes time → time-consuming. Some are now written as one word.

time-consumingeye-catchingrecord-breakingmouth-wateringheart-warminglaw-abidingaward-winningthought-provokingbreathtakingheartbreaking
  • a film that makes you think → a thought-provoking film
  • citizens who obey (abide by) the law → law-abiding citizens
F

Noun / self + past participle

self + taught → self-taught

Rule: passive meaning, and the noun tells us who or what did it (by…), or how: made at home → home-made; taught by yourself → self-taught.

self-taughtself-madeself-employedhome-made / homemadehand-mademan-madesun-driedtongue-tiedstar-struckheartbroken
  • He became rich through his own hard work. → He is a self-made man.
  • I was so nervous I couldn't speak. → I was tongue-tied.
G

Noun + adjective

world + famous → world-famous

Rule: the noun makes the adjective more specific, or works as a comparison (ice-cold = as cold as ice). Some second elements are very productive: -free, -friendly, -proof, -conscious.

world-famousice-coldcrystal-clearsugar-freeduty-freeuser-friendlyeco-friendlycamera-shyhealth-consciousself-consciouswaterproofbulletproof
  • She doesn't like being photographed. → She is camera-shy.
  • an app that is easy to use → a user-friendly app
H

Number + noun (singular)

ten + minute → a ten-minute walk

Rule: used for measurements, time, age and quantity before a noun. The noun stays singular, and all the parts are joined with hyphens.

a two-day coursea five-star hotela 300-page novela three-hour exama 17-year-old studenta second-hand cara first-class ticket
  • The walk takes ten minutes. → It's a ten-minute walk. (not ten-minutes)
  • My brother is seventeen years old. → He is a seventeen-year-old student.
💡 After the verb, there are no hyphens and the plural returns: The student is seventeen years old.

Spelling & grammar rules

Six rules that examiners check, and that make the difference at C1.

  1. Hyphenate before a noun. When the compound comes before the noun, use a hyphen: a well-known writer, an up-to-date map.
  2. After a verb, the hyphen is often dropped with adverb + participle and number compounds: The writer is well known. The map is up to date. But compounds that are fixed adjectives keep it everywhere: She is very level-headed. He is narrow-minded.
  3. No hyphen after an -ly adverb: a highly respected doctor, a highly-respected doctor. The -ly already shows the word is an adverb.
  4. The noun stays singular in number compounds and -ed compounds: a two-week holiday, a two-weeks holiday; a green-eyed girl.
  5. Comparatives and superlatives change the first part when it is good, well, bad, long, etc.: well-known → better-known → the best-known; good-looking → better-looking; long-lasting → longer-lasting. With the -minded/-hearted type we normally use more / most: more open-minded, the most kind-hearted.
  6. You can form nouns with -ness: absent-minded → absent-mindedness, open-minded → open-mindedness, kind-hearted → kind-heartedness, self-conscious → self-consciousness. Adverbs with -ly: absent-mindedly, half-heartedly.

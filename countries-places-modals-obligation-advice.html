💡 Watch out: easily confused words

  • country (a state) · the country / the countryside (rural areas, not towns) · land (ground; also a poetic word for country).
  • nation (people) · nationality (legal) · citizen (a person with rights in a state).
  • Slovakia (country) · Slovak (adjective, language, person) · the Slovaks (people).
  • sight (sightseeing, a famous place) · site (a place where sth is or was: a building site, a World Heritage Site) · view (what you see from a place).
  • Gothic, Baroque, Renaissance, Romanesque are always written with a capital letter.
  • situated in a town / country · on a river / the coast / a hill · at the foot of a mountain.

📰 Reading: Slovakia – Small Country, Big Surprises

A guide to Slovakia for visitors, full of rules and advice. You will analyse the modal verbs in Exercise 7.

1Slovakia is a small landlocked country in the heart of Europe, but it packs an incredible variety into just 49,000 square kilometres. In the north, the High Tatras rise to over 2,600 metres; in the south, the fertile Danubian Lowland is covered in fields of sunflowers and vineyards. In between, you'll find castles, caves, spa towns and wooden churches, many of which are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

2If you are visiting from abroad, there are a few things you need to know. Citizens of EU countries don't have to show a passport at the border, but you must carry a valid ID card at all times. On public transport you have to validate your ticket as soon as you get on, or you might get a fine. And in the mountains, you mustn't leave the marked trails in the national parks – the rules are strict, and rescue operations are expensive.

3Some advice from the locals: you should try bryndzové halušky, potato dumplings with sheep's cheese, even if it doesn't sound tempting. You'd better book accommodation in the Tatras well in advance in winter, because hotels are often fully booked. And you ought to learn at least "Ďakujem" – thank you. People appreciate the effort.

4Of course, not everything is perfect. Some regions in the east are still struggling with unemployment, and many young people have moved abroad. Strange as it may seem, some Slovaks know Croatia's beaches better than their own mountains. While I understand the attraction of the sea, I think we should discover our own country first.

5When I asked my grandmother what visitors were supposed to see in our town, she laughed. "In my day, we didn't need to travel," she said. "Everything was a stone's throw from home." Perhaps she was right: sometimes the best places are the ones right in front of us.

📏 Modal Verbs of Obligation and Necessity

Rules, laws, things you need to do – and things you don't. The difference between mustn't and don't have to is a Maturita favourite.

MeaningPresentPastExample
obligation – rules, laws, the speaker's opinionmusthad toYou must wear a helmet. (rule / I think it's important)
obligation – from outside, circumstanceshave to / have got tohad toI have to change trains in Žilina.
prohibition – it is forbiddenmustn't / can't / be not allowed tocouldn't / wasn't allowed toYou mustn't feed the bears.
no obligation – it isn't necessarydon't have to / don't need to / needn'tdidn't have to / didn't need toYou don't have to book a guided tour.
necessityneed toneeded toYou need to buy a vignette for the motorway.
permissioncan / be allowed to / may (formal)could / was allowed toVisitors may take photos without flash.
what people expect / rules often brokenbe supposed towas supposed toYou're supposed to stay on the path.

⚠️ mustn't ≠ don't have to

mustn't = DON'T do it! · don't have to = you can do it, but it isn't necessary
You mustn't swim in the lake – it's dangerous. (forbidden)
You don't have to swim – you can just sunbathe. (not necessary)

🕐 must has no past or future form

past → had to · future → will have to · infinitive → to have to
We had to queue for two hours. · You'll have to show your ID. · I hate having to get up early.

⚠️ Typical mistakes

  • Yesterday I must work. → Yesterday I had to work.
  • You mustn't pay – it's free. → You don't have to pay – it's free.
  • Do I must show my passport? → Do I have to show my passport? / Must I show…?
  • You needn't to come. → You needn't come. / You don't need to come.

💡 Giving Advice

From a friendly suggestion to a serious warning: choose the right strength.

StructureStrengthExample
should / ought to + infinitivenormal adviceYou should visit the Slovak Paradise. · You ought to try the local wine.
had better ('d better) + infinitivestrong advice, a warning about nowYou'd better take a jacket – it gets cold in the mountains.
had better notwarningYou'd better not be late, or you'll miss the bus.
If I were you, I'd …personal adviceIf I were you, I'd go to Košice instead.
Why don't you … ? / You could …suggestionWhy don't you take the train?
It's (high) time you + pastit should happen nowIt's high time you visited your grandparents.
should have / shouldn't have + past participlecriticism of the pastYou should have booked earlier.

⚠️ Typical mistakes

  • You had better to go. → You had better go. (no to)
  • You don't had better… → You'd better not…
  • You ought go. → You ought to go.
  • If I was you… → If I were you… (formal, recommended in exams)

💬 Fixed Expressions About Places

Use one or two of these in the speaking exam to sound natural.

Near and far

  • a stone's throw from = very close to
  • in the middle of nowhere / in the back of beyond = very far from towns
  • off the beaten track = away from tourist routes
  • just round the corner = very near

Home

  • home sweet home = it's nice to be home
  • there's no place like home
  • to feel at home = to feel relaxed and comfortable
  • to make yourself at home = to relax as if at home

Travel & places

  • when in Rome (do as the Romans do) = follow local customs
  • Rome wasn't built in a day = big things take time
  • paint the town red = go out and celebrate
  • a tourist trap = an overpriced place full of tourists
  • the world is your oyster = you can go anywhere and do anything

⚖️ Expressing a Contrasting Opinion – C1 Level

You already know however and although. These structures will impress the examiner.

🚀 C1 ways to contrast

Adjective / adverb + as + subject + verb, … · Much as + clause, … · While / Whilst …, … · Granted, … but …
Strange as it may seem, I prefer villages to big cities.
Much as I love Bratislava, I couldn't live there.
While I agree that tourism brings money, it also damages historic towns.
Granted, the old town is beautiful, but it's full of tourist traps.
Be that as it may, we should protect our heritage. (formal = even if that's true)

🗨️ Presenting the opposite view in an essay

Some people argue that … However, … · It is often claimed that … , but in reality … · Opponents of this view point out that … · Contrary to popular belief, …

Discussion Lab

Three questions about places

Work in pairs or small groups. Choose one question, add your own arguments and reach a conclusion together.

🏙️ City or village?

+ City: jobs, culture, public transport

− City: noise, pollution, high rents

+ Village: nature, peace, community

− Village: few amenities, long commutes

🧳 Should tourists pay to enter historic towns?

+ Money for restoration, fewer crowds

− Unfair to poorer travellers

+ Protects locals' quality of life

− Hurts local businesses

🇸🇰 Discover your own country first?

+ Cheaper, supports local economy

− Abroad you learn languages and cultures

+ Shorter journeys, less pollution

− No sea in Slovakia!

🗨️ Useful phrases

  • Describing a place: It's situated in … · It's famous for … · It dates back to … · It's a stone's throw from …
  • Giving advice to visitors: You really should … · You'd better … · If I were you, I'd … · You don't have to …, but …
  • Contrasting: Much as I like …, … · While I agree that …, … · Granted, … but …

Your town

  • What would you change about it?

Geography

  • Describe the landscape of your region.
  • Which natural places in Slovakia are a must-see?
  • How is climate change affecting your area?

Heritage

  • Which landmark are you most proud of?
  • Why is it important to protect historic buildings?
  • Can tourism destroy the places people love?

💡 Watch out: easily confused names

  • England ≠ Great Britain ≠ the United Kingdom. A Scot is British, but not English!
  • the UK, the USA, the Netherlands take the · Britain, England, America, Ireland don't.
  • British (adjective) · a Briton / a Brit (person) · the British (people) · English (the language).
  • Big Ben is the bell; the tower is called the Elizabeth Tower.
  • Holland is part of the Netherlands – just like England is part of the UK.
  • a public school in the UK is private; in the USA it is a state school!

📰 Reading: Are the British Really Like That?

A student's year in Manchester, full of the grammar of this lesson – you will find it in Exercise 7.

1Ask a foreign student what the British are like, and you will probably hear the same answers: they drink tea, talk about the weather, queue politely and never show their feelings. But are these stereotypes true? I spent a year in Manchester in order to find out.

2Some of them, admittedly, are not completely wrong. People really do talk about the weather a lot – not because they find it fascinating, but because it is a safe topic for small talk. As the weather changes so quickly, there is always something new to say. Having lived there for a few months, I started doing it myself.

3Other clichés turned out to be completely false. I had expected people to be cold and reserved, but my neighbours were incredibly friendly. They invited me for dinner during my first week so that I wouldn't feel lonely. When I asked them why they were so kind, they simply said, "That's what neighbours are for."

4What surprised me most was how multicultural the country is. My classmates came from Pakistan, Poland, Nigeria and Jamaica, and many of them spoke English with a strong Mancunian accent. Due to centuries of empire and immigration, "British" no longer describes one type of person. In fact, I often wondered whether a "typical Brit" really exists.

5So, are stereotypes useful? They can help us to prepare for a new culture, but they can also stop us from seeing real people. My advice: go there with an open mind, ask questions, and be ready to change your opinions. And take an umbrella, just in case. That stereotype is true.

🔗 Linking Expressions and Phrasal Verbs

Connect your ideas clearly and talk about travel and culture like a native speaker.

Adding

  • moreover, furthermore, in addition (to)
  • besides, what is more
  • not only … but also
  • as well as + noun / -ing

Sequencing

  • first of all, to begin with
  • then, after that, subsequently
  • finally, lastly, last but not least

Giving examples

  • for example, for instance
  • such as + noun
  • in particular, especially

Result

  • so, therefore, as a result
  • consequently, thus (formal)
  • that's why

Phrasal verbs for travel and culture

❓ Indirect Questions

The polite way to ask questions – essential when you travel and in the speaking exam role-play.

Direct question

Where is the station?

What time does the museum open?

Is it far?

Who wrote this play?

Indirect question (more polite)

Could you tell me where the station is?

Do you know what time the museum opens?

I wonder if / whether it is far.

Do you know who wrote this play? (subject question – no change)

📏 Three rules

1. statement word order (subject + verb) · 2. no do / does / did · 3. yes/no questions → if / whether
Typical openings: Could you tell me… · Do you know… · I'd like to know… · I wonder… · Do you have any idea… · Can you remember… · I'm not sure…
The question mark depends on the opening: Do you know where it is? but I wonder where it is.

⚠️ Typical mistakes

  • Could you tell me where is the bank? → Could you tell me where the bank is?
  • Do you know what time does the train leave? → Do you know what time the train leaves?
  • I wonder is it open. → I wonder if / whether it is open.
  • Can you tell me how much costs it? → Can you tell me how much it costs?

🎨 Adjectives and Adverbs

How to form adverbs, which adjectives have no adverb, and why hardly is not the adverb of hard.

Forming adverbs

  • quick → quickly · careful → carefully
  • happy → happily · easy → easily
  • terrible → terribly · gentle → gently
  • basic → basically · dramatic → dramatically (but public → publicly)
  • good → well · fast → fast · hard → hard

Adjectives ending in -ly

  • friendly, lovely, lonely, silly, ugly, lively, elderly
  • No adverb! Use in a … way:
  • She smiled in a friendly way.

Same form, different meaning

  • hard (with effort) · hardly (almost not)
  • late (not on time) · lately (recently)
  • high (up) · highly (very: highly recommended)
  • near (close) · nearly (almost)
  • free (no cost) · freely (without limits)

-ed or -ing?

  • -ed = how you feel: I was bored.
  • -ing = what causes it: The tour was boring.
  • interested / interesting, confused / confusing, amazed / amazing

🎨 Order of adjectives

opinion – size – age – shape – colour – origin – material – purpose + NOUN
a lovely little old stone cottage · a beautiful red Scottish woollen scarf · an enormous Gothic cathedral

👃 Linking verbs take ADJECTIVES

be · look · seem · feel · sound · smell · taste · become · get
The fish and chips smell good. (not well) · You look tired. · That sounds great!

🧭 Clauses of Reason and Purpose

Why did it happen? What did you do it for? Two questions, two sets of linkers.

🧭 Reason (why?)

  • because / as / since + clause: As it was raining, we visited a museum.
  • because of / due to / owing to / on account of + noun / -ing: The flight was cancelled due to fog.
  • due to the fact that + clause (formal)
  • for (literary): He went home, for he was tired.

🎯 Purpose (what for?)

  • to + infinitive: I went to London to improve my English.
  • in order to / so as to (more formal): …in order to find out.
  • negative: in order not to / so as not to: We left early so as not to miss the train.
  • so that + can / could / will / would: I saved money so that I could travel.
  • for + noun / -ing (purpose of a thing): a knife for cutting bread · We went out for a walk.
  • in case = because sth might happen: Take an umbrella in case it rains.

⚠️ Typical mistakes

  • I went to London for improve my English. → I went to London to improve my English.
  • I came here for to study. → I came here to study.
  • Because of it was late, … → Because it was late, … / Because of the late hour, …
  • …in order to not miss… → …in order not to miss…
  • Take a coat in case it will rain. → …in case it rains.

✂️ Shortening Subordinate Clauses with -ing (Revision)

A quick revision to make your writing shorter and more advanced.

✂️ Revision: shortening subordinate clauses with -ing

same subject → -ing (at the same time) · Having + 3rd form (earlier) · Not + -ing (negative)
While I was walking along the Royal Mile, I heard bagpipes. → Walking along the Royal Mile, I heard bagpipes.
As I didn't know the way, I asked a policeman. → Not knowing the way, I asked a policeman.
After we had visited the castle, we went to a pub. → Having visited the castle, we went to a pub.

🎭 Are stereotypes ever useful?

+ They prepare you for cultural differences

− They create prejudice

+ They can be funny and harmless

− They stop you seeing real people

🇬🇧 or 🇺🇸 – which English should you learn?

+ British: the Maturita standard, closer to Europe

− American: films, music, the internet

+ Mixing is fine if you're consistent

− Spelling differences in exams

🎉 Should Slovaks celebrate Halloween?

+ Fun, creative, good for children

− Commercial, replaces our own traditions

+ Cultural exchange

− All Saints' Day is a quiet family day here

🗨️ Useful phrases

  • Polite questions: Could you tell me …? · Do you happen to know …? · I was wondering if …
  • Reason and purpose: I'd like to go there in order to … · Owing to …, … · so that I could …
  • Comparing cultures: Unlike in Slovakia, … · Whereas we …, the British … · It's similar to our …
Speaking Exam · Questions

Answer in 1–2 minutes each

Geography & history

  • What are the parts of the UK?
  • Which period of British or American history interests you most?
  • How did English become a global language?

People & life

  • What are British / American people like?
  • Which stereotypes are true or false?
  • Would you like to study or work abroad?

Sights

  • Which place in the English-speaking world would you most like to visit?
  • Plan a three-day trip to London.
  • Compare a British sight with a Slovak one.

Customs

  • Describe a British, Irish or American festival.
  • Compare Christmas in Britain and Slovakia.
  • Which tradition would you bring to Slovakia?

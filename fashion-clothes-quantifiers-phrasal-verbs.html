💡 Watch out: easily confused words

  • suit = looks good on you (style, colour) · fit = is the right size · match / go with = looks good with sth else.
  • wear = have on your body (She's wearing a dress.) · put on = the action of dressing · dress = put clothes on sb / yourself (She dressed quickly.) · get dressed = put on your clothes.
  • clothes (always plural, no singular) · cloth = material · clothing = clothes in general (formal, uncountable).
  • trousers, jeans, shorts, pyjamas, tights are plural: These jeans are tight. One item = a pair of jeans.
  • jewellery is uncountable: a piece of jewellery, never a jewellery.

📰 Reading: The Real Price of Fashion

Read about fast fashion and its hidden costs. Notice the quantifiers and contrast linkers – you will analyse them in Exercise 7.

1Every year, the world buys about 100 billion new pieces of clothing. Many of them are worn only a few times before they end up in a bin. Fast fashion has made trendy clothes cheaper than ever, but critics say that we are paying a great deal more than the price on the label.

2The environmental cost is enormous. Producing a single pair of jeans uses several thousand litres of water, and synthetic fabrics release tiny plastic fibres every time we wash them. Very little of our old clothing is recycled into new garments; most of it is burned or ends up in landfill, often in countries that have nothing to do with the fashion industry.

3There is also a human cost. In 2013, more than 1,100 workers died when the Rana Plaza building, which housed several clothes factories, collapsed in Bangladesh. Since then, a few brands have improved conditions, but many workers still earn too little to live on. Admittedly, the industry provides jobs for millions of people; however, a job should not put your life at risk.

4On the other hand, young people are starting to change their habits. Second-hand apps have become hugely popular, and plenty of teenagers now prefer vintage shopping to the high street. Some even organise swap parties, where nobody pays anything: everyone brings clothes they no longer wear and takes home something "new".

5Fashion is not the enemy, of course. Clothes allow us to express ourselves, and few things feel better than a perfect outfit. Nevertheless, the question we should ask is not "What's in fashion?" but "Do I really need it?" Neither the brands nor the influencers will ask it for us.

🔢 Quantifiers: How Much? How Many?

Countable or uncountable? Positive or negative? Two things or more? Three questions that decide the right quantifier.

With countable nouns (plural)With uncountable nounsWith both
many clothes · a few shirts · few people · several shops · a (large) number of brands · both · each / every + singularmuch money · a little time · little water · a great deal of waste · a (large) amount of fabricsome · any · a lot of / lots of · plenty of · no · none of · all · most · enough · more · less (unc.) / fewer (count.)

⚖️ a few / few · a little / little

a few / a little = some (positive) · few / little = almost none (negative)
I've got a few nice dresses. (some – good) · Few people can afford haute couture. (hardly anyone)
There's a little money left. · There's little hope of finding it.

👥 both · either · neither · none · all

2 things: both / either / neither · 3+ things: all / any / none
Both dresses are lovely. · You can wear either (of them). · Neither of them fits. · None of my five jackets is warm enough.
neither … nor: Neither the shirt nor the tie matches. · either … or: Pay either in cash or by card.

☝️ each vs every

every = all of a group (3+), always + singular noun: Every student wears a uniform.
each = individually, also for 2: She wore a ring on each hand. · Each of the models was paid €500.

⚠️ Typical mistakes

  • I haven't got many money. → I haven't got much money.
  • Less people buy new clothes. → Fewer people buy new clothes. (formal; less is common in speech)
  • Neither of them don't fit. → Neither of them fits. (no double negative)
  • Every students → Every student
  • All of us didn't like it. → None of us liked it.
  • In positive sentences, much / many sound formal: I've got a lot of clothes. is more natural than I've got many clothes.

👕 Phrasal Verbs: Clothes and Fashion

You can't talk about clothes without them. Remember: with a pronoun, separable verbs split – try it on, not try on it.

Phrasal verbMeaningExample

💬 Fixed Expressions and Idioms

Native speakers use these all the time. One well-placed idiom in the speaking exam shows a C1 level.

Looking good

  • dressed to kill / dressed to the nines = wearing very elegant clothes
  • fit like a glove = fit perfectly
  • look a million dollars = look wonderful
  • the height of fashion = very fashionable

Fashion & trends

  • in vogue / all the rage = very fashionable now
  • out of date / old hat = no longer fashionable
  • come back into fashion = be popular again
  • keep up with the Joneses = buy things to look as rich as your neighbours

Clothes idioms

  • on a shoestring = with very little money
  • hand-me-downs = clothes from an older sibling
  • put yourself in sb's shoes = imagine their situation
  • keep it under your hat = keep it secret
  • at the drop of a hat = immediately, without hesitating
  • if the cap fits, wear it = if the criticism is true, accept it

⚖️ Expressing a Contrasting Opinion

Balanced essays and good discussions need contrast. Learn the grammar behind each linker.

LinkerGrammarExample
but / yetjoins two clausesFast fashion is cheap, but it harms the planet.
although / even though / though+ clause (subject + verb)Although the dress was expensive, I bought it.
despite / in spite of+ noun / -ing / the fact thatDespite the price, I bought it. · In spite of being expensive, …
however / nevertheless / nonethelessnew sentence, followed by a commaVintage clothes are unique. However, they are hard to find.
whereas / whilecompares two different factsMy sister loves designer labels, whereas I prefer second-hand.
on the other handintroduces the opposite argumentOn the one hand, … On the other hand, …
admittedly / it is true that … butaccept a point, then disagreeAdmittedly, uniforms are boring, but they save money.
even so / all the sameinformal, after a factIt's a bit tight. Even so, I love it.

🗨️ Disagreeing politely in a discussion

I see your point, but … · That may be true, however … · I'm not sure I agree. · I take your point, but have you considered …? · With respect, I think … · I'm afraid I see it differently.

⚠️ Typical mistakes

  • Despite it was expensive, … → Despite the fact that it was expensive, … / Although it was expensive, …
  • In spite the rain, … → In spite of the rain, …
  • Although it was cheap, but I didn't buy it. → Although it was cheap, I didn't buy it. (never both!)
  • It's cheap, however it's low quality. → It's cheap. However, it's low quality. / It's cheap; however, it's low quality.

🗣️ Speaking & Writing

Exam-style tasks. In the essay, aim for three contrast linkers, three quantifiers and eight words from the cards.

Discussion Lab

Three fashion debates

Work in pairs or small groups. Choose one debate, add your own arguments and reach a conclusion together.

🏫 Should all schools have uniforms?

+ No competition over brands, saves time

− Students can't express their personality

+ A sense of belonging

− Expensive for some families

💸 Should fast fashion be taxed?

+ Less waste, better quality

− Poorer families couldn't afford clothes

+ Money for recycling

− Jobs in factories might disappear

📱 Do influencers have too much power?

+ They inspire creativity and confidence

− They encourage overconsumption

+ Small brands become visible

− Unrealistic body images

🗨️ Useful phrases

  • Contrasting: I see your point, but … · Admittedly, …, however … · On the other hand, …
  • Describing clothes in a photo: She's wearing a floral dress which goes with her … · He looks dressed to kill. · It might be a formal occasion.
  • Quantities: Few people … · A great deal of … · Neither of them …

The History of Ballet

2Learn

Ballet is a type of dance performed on a theatre stage, usually telling a story through movement and music, without words. Dancers train for many years to master special steps and positions.

Timeline: How ballet began

15th–16th century — Ballet began at Renaissance courts in Italy, as elegant dances performed at royal parties.
1661 — In France, King Louis XIV (who loved to dance himself) founded the first ballet school, the Académie Royale de Danse.
1700s–1800s — Ballet moved from royal courts to public theatres. Dancers began to wear the tutu and dance en pointe (on the tips of their toes).
Late 1800s — Russia became the new home of great ballet. Choreographer Marius Petipa created famous ballets in St Petersburg, often with music by Tchaikovsky.
Early 1900s — The company Ballets Russes brought modern, daring ballets to Paris, including works by Stravinsky.

🎭 Who makes a ballet?

A composer writes the music. A choreographer invents the dance steps. The corps de ballet is the group of dancers who dance together, while a prima ballerina is the leading female dancer.

👗 What is a tutu?

A tutu is the short, stiff skirt worn by ballerinas. It lets the audience see the dancer's legs clearly during difficult steps and jumps.

3Famous Ballets & Composers

Here are five of the most important ballets in history.

Swan Lake
Composer: Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky · 1877, Russia

A princess named Odette is turned into a swan by an evil sorcerer's curse. A prince falls in love with her, but the sorcerer tricks him — can true love break the spell?

Famous for its beautiful, sad main melody and the dancers' arm movements, which look like a swan's wings.

The Nutcracker
Composer: Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky · 1892, Russia

On Christmas Eve, a girl named Clara receives a nutcracker doll as a gift. At midnight it comes to life, defeats the Mouse King, and takes Clara to a magical land of sweets.

A very popular Christmas ballet, famous for the "Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy" and the "Waltz of the Flowers."

Giselle
Composer: Adolphe Adam · 1841, France

A village girl named Giselle dies of a broken heart after learning her lover already has a fiancée. Her ghost joins a group of spirits, but her love protects him from their revenge.

One of the oldest ballets still performed today — a classic example of the Romantic style.

Romeo and Juliet
Composer: Sergei Prokofiev · 1935, Soviet Union

Based on Shakespeare's play: two young people from rival families fall in love in the Italian city of Verona, with a tragic ending.

Famous for the powerful, marching "Dance of the Knights" theme.

The Rite of Spring
Composer: Igor Stravinsky · 1913, France

Dancers act out an ancient pagan ceremony: a village celebrates the arrival of spring, and a young girl is chosen to dance herself to death as a sacrifice.

Famous for causing a scandal at its premiere — the strange rhythms and steps shocked the Paris audience!

5Listen

🎵 Extract 1 — "Swan Theme" from Swan Lake (Tchaikovsky)
London Philharmonic Orchestra
Listen and think:
  1. Is the melody happy or sad? What feeling does it give you?
  2. Can you hear one instrument playing the main tune? Which family does it belong to — strings, brass, or woodwind?
🎵 Extract 2 — "Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy" from The Nutcracker (Tchaikovsky)
The Royal Ballet — with dancers
Listen and think:
  1. This piece uses a special bell-like instrument called the celesta. Can you hear it?
  2. Watch the ballerina's movements. Do they look light and delicate, or heavy and strong?
🎵 Extract 3 — "Augurs of Spring" from The Rite of Spring (Stravinsky)
London Symphony Orchestra, conducted by Leonard Bernstein
Listen and think:
  1. Is this music similar to or very different from the first two extracts? How?
  2. Do you understand why this music shocked audiences in Paris in 1913?

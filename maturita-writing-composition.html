Part 1

The exam at a glance

The composition is the written form of the internal part of the Maturita (PFIČ). It is written on the same days as the external test (EČ), but it is marked by your own teachers using national criteria from NIVAM.

60 min
time for level B2 (90 min for C1)
200–220
words for B2 — under 120 words = 0 points
1 task
a set title, genre and 3 content points (older tasks: 5)
20 pts
4 criteria × 5 points
Remember: no dictionaries, notebooks or textbooks. You get stamped paper for a draft (koncept), but only the official answer sheet is marked. Write in joined handwriting, or make capital and small letters clearly different — unclear capitals count as spelling mistakes.

How your composition is marked

CriterionWhat the examiner checksHow to get 5 points
I. Content
Obsah textu		Does the text answer the task? Are all content points covered?Develop every bullet point clearly and in detail. A missing or thin point costs points straight away.
II. Organisation
Členenie a stavba textu		Genre features, paragraphs, logical links between ideasTitle, introduction, one paragraph per bullet point, conclusion. Link ideas with a variety of connectors.
III. Grammar
Morfológia, syntax, pravopis		Accuracy, range of structures, spellingMix simple and complex sentences: relative clauses, conditionals, passive, participle clauses, inversion. Errors should be rare.
IV. Vocabulary
Slovná zásoba		Range and accuracy of words and collocationsRich, topic-specific vocabulary, correct collocations, no repetition of good, bad, very, a lot of.

Word-count rules

  • Every word counts — articles, prepositions, conjunctions and numbers written as words.
  • 120–199 words: the shortage lowers your marks in all four criteria.
  • Writing more than 220 words is not penalised, but a long, rambling text usually contains more mistakes. Aim for 210.
Part 2

Structure: one paragraph per job

The safest plan for every PFIČ genre is five paragraphs. Each content point from the task gets its own body paragraph — then the examiner can tick them off easily.

TitleCopy the title from the task (or, in an article, write a catchy one).—
IntroductionHook the reader, introduce the topic in your own words and state your opinion or aim.~35 words
Body 1Content point 1 — topic sentence + explanation + example.~45 words
Body 2Content point 2 — topic sentence + explanation + example.~45 words
Body 3Content point 3 — often personal: your experience, plan or vision.~45 words
ConclusionSum up, restate your opinion in new words, finish with a final thought, recommendation or question.~35 words

Which genre? Know the differences

Opinion essay

Formal. State your view in the introduction and support it with reasons and examples. Restate it in the conclusion.

“In my view…”, “I firmly believe…”, “To sum up, …”

For-and-against / balanced essay

Formal and objective. Present both sides fairly; give your opinion only in the conclusion.

“On the one hand…”, “Critics argue that…”, “On balance, …”

Article (school magazine)

Semi-formal and lively. Catchy title, address the reader directly, rhetorical questions, end with advice or a question.

“Have you ever wondered…?”, “Why not…?”

In all three genres: no contractions in essays (do not, not don't), no Hi everyone! or Bye!, and no lists with bullet points — write full paragraphs.
Part 3

Advanced linking words

Linking words hold your composition together and are checked under Organisation. Words in green are especially good at an advanced level. Vary them — never start three sentences with Also.

For the introduction

Introducing the topic

It is often said that…In recent years, there has been a growing debate about…Few would deny that…Nowadays,…These days,…Hardly a day goes by without…

Hardly a day goes by without a new study on the effects of social media.

Stating your aim or opinion

This essay will examine…In my view,…I would argue that…I think that…It is my firm belief that…

In my view, however, the two do not necessarily exclude each other.

For the body paragraphs

Adding ideas

Moreover,Furthermore,What is more,In addition,Not only… but also…Besides,

Reading has not only broadened my vocabulary but also taught me to see the world differently.

Contrasting

However,Nevertheless,Nonetheless,On the other hand,whereas / whilealthough / even thoughdespite / in spite of + noun/-ingBy contrast,

Paper books are heavy, whereas an e-reader can store thousands of titles.

Cause and effect

Consequently,As a result,Therefore,…, which is why…owing to / due to + nounbecause / since / asThis leads to…

Imported goods tend to be cheaper, which is why many families choose them.

Giving examples

For example,For instance,such asA case in point is…To illustrate,namely

A case in point is my aunt, who runs a thriving architecture studio.

Sequencing

First and foremost,First of all, / To begin with,Secondly,Subsequently,Finally,Last but not least,

First and foremost, I have always been fascinated by wild nature.

Emphasising & conceding

Indeed,In fact,Above all,It is worth noting that…Admittedly,It cannot be denied that…Even so,

It cannot be denied that work has a significant influence on family life.

Condition

provided thatas long asunlessotherwisein case

A career and a family can go hand in hand, provided that we set clear priorities.

Referring to a point

As far as… is concerned,When it comes to…,As for…,With regard to…,

When it comes to electronics, foreign brands usually offer a wider choice.

For the conclusion

Summing up

All things considered,Taking everything into account,On balance,To sum up, / To conclude,In conclusion,All in all,Weighing up both sides,

All things considered, Lofoten is far more than a holiday destination for me.

Upgrade your linkers

Instead of (B1)Try (B2+)Watch the grammar
Also, …Moreover, / What is more,Comma after the linker; start of a sentence.
But …However, / Nevertheless,However starts a new sentence and needs a comma.
So …Consequently, / …, which is why…which is why follows a comma, inside one sentence.
Although it was expensive, but we bought it.Although it was expensive, we bought it.Never use although and but together.
Despite of the rain / Despite it rainedDespite the rain / Despite the fact that it raineddespite / in spite of + noun or -ing, never + clause.
In the other handOn the other handAlways on. Use it after On the one hand or a contrasting idea.
Firstly of allFirst of all, / Firstly,Choose one — not a mixture.
In my opinion, I think that…In my opinion, … / I believe that…Don't say the same thing twice.
At the end, …In the end, / Finally, / In conclusion,At the end of = the last part of something (at the end of the film).
Part 4

Sentence starters

Ready-made beginnings for each part of the composition. Learn five or six you like and practise adapting them to any topic.

Introduction — hooks

  • Rhetorical question: Have you ever wondered what life would be like without…?
  • General statement: In today's fast-paced world, more and more people…
  • Scene: Walk into any … and you will see…
  • Contrast: Not so long ago, … Today, however, …
  • Common view: It is widely believed that… But is that really the case?

Introduction — thesis

  • In this essay, I would like to explain why…
  • In my view, there are several good reasons for this trend.
  • I would argue that the advantages clearly outweigh the disadvantages.
  • Although opinions differ, I am convinced that…
  • Let us take a closer look at both sides of the argument.

Body — topic sentences

  • One of the main reasons why … is that…
  • Another important factor to consider is…
  • As far as … is concerned, there is no simple answer.
  • When it comes to …, the situation is rather different.
  • There are strong arguments in favour of / against…

Body — examples & personal experience

  • A good example of this is…
  • From my own experience, I can say that…
  • Last summer, for instance, I…
  • I will never forget the day when…
  • Someone who has always inspired me is…, who…

Body — the other side

  • Critics, however, argue that…
  • Admittedly, … Even so, …
  • It cannot be denied that…; nevertheless, …
  • On the other hand, it would be unrealistic to…
  • Some people claim that… , but I am not convinced.

Body — future, plans & hypothesis

  • As for my own future, I would like to…
  • If I had the chance to…, I would…
  • In ten years' time, I hope I will have…
  • I intend to … , perhaps by …-ing
  • Were I in their position, I would… (inversion — very advanced)

Conclusion

  • All things considered, I firmly believe that…
  • Taking everything into account, the best approach is…
  • On balance, the benefits seem to outweigh the drawbacks.
  • To sum up, … can go hand in hand, provided that…
  • Only time will tell whether…

Article — talking to the reader

  • Are … really going out of fashion?
  • Believe it or not, …
  • Let's face it: …
  • So, next time you …, why not … instead?
  • Give it a try — you might be surprised!

Part 7

Weak vs strong: the same task, two answers

Maturita 2023
Nature and us · Essay for a school magazine competition

Write an essay (200–220 words) entitled Nowadays People Like Enjoying Nature. Include:

  • the reasons for the increasing popularity of agro-tourism (tourism in the countryside)
  • the possibilities of enjoying nature and agro-tourism in Slovakia
  • your contribution to nature protection in your region
Weak answer · about 8/20
Nowadays People Like Enjoying Nature

Examiner's comments

  • Content 2/5: point 3 (your contribution to nature protection) is completely missing; point 2 is only a list of places.
  • Organisation 2/5: no paragraphs, informal greeting and “Bye!”, the same linker (Also) three times, one very long run-on sentence.
  • Grammar 2/5: frequent basic errors (more better, there is many, tourists comes), only simple sentences.
  • Vocabulary 2/5: repetitive (very, many, also), informal (super cool), wrong words (home cheese).
  • Length: 176 words — below the 200–220 range.
Strong answer · 20/20
Nowadays People Like Enjoying Nature

Examiner's comments

  • Content 5/5: each point has its own paragraph with explanation and a personal example.
  • Organisation 5/5: clear introduction and conclusion, varied linkers (First of all, Moreover, for instance, however, To conclude, provided that).
  • Grammar 5/5: present perfect continuous, relative clauses, conditional, gerund subject (Spending a few days… allows…).
  • Vocabulary 5/5: topic words and collocations: on the rise, picturesque regions, clean-up events, rediscover nature.
  • Length: 217 words.

Warm-up: What do you already know?

Click an answer for each question — no wrong start, just a first guess before we dive in.

1. Look at the pictures. Which instrument is from the Baroque time?

The harpsichord is the Baroque instrument. The piano came later, in the Classical time.

2. Gregorian chant has...

Gregorian chant has one melody line. There is no music under it. One voice, or many voices together, sing the same line.

3. Sonata form is normal in which time?

Sonata form started in Classicism. Composers used it for the first part of symphonies, sonatas, and quartets.

Step 2

First Return — Prehistory and the Middle Ages

Prvý návrat – Pravek, stredovek

  • Prehistoric people did not write music down. They played by ear. Music was for rituals, hunting, and talking to each other.
  • The first instruments were bone flutes and simple drums.
  • The Middle Ages was about 500–1450. The Church was very important.
  • Gregorian chant is church music. It has one voice and one melody. There is no music under it. It is in Latin.
  • First, music had one melody line (monophony). Later, music had two or more lines together (this is called organum, an early polyphony).
  • Guido of Arezzo made a new way to write music, in the 11th century. Now people could read and learn music.
  • Troubadours and minstrels sang songs outside the church. They sang about love. They played the lute, harp, or vielle.

Step 3

Second Return — The Baroque

Druhý návrat – barok (c. 1600–1750)

  • Baroque music has many decorations (ornaments). It is dramatic.
  • Basso continuo is a bass line under the music. It plays all the time (harpsichord + cello or bassoon).
  • The music goes suddenly from loud to soft. This is called terraced dynamics — not a slow change.
  • Music uses major and minor keys. This was new.
  • New types of music start now: opera, oratorio, cantata, concerto, fugue, sonata.
  • The harpsichord and the organ were the main keyboard instruments.
  • Famous composers: J. S. Bach, Handel, Vivaldi, Monteverdi, Purcell.

Step 4

Third Return — Classicism

Tretí návrat – Klasicizmus (c. 1750–1820)

  • Classical music is clear, simple, and balanced. This is different from Baroque music.
  • One melody is important. Other instruments play with it. This is called homophony.
  • Music could get louder (crescendo) or quieter (diminuendo) slowly. This was new.
  • The symphony and the string quartet became very popular. The orchestra had a normal size now.
  • Sonata form became the normal shape for the first part of music (see Step 5).
  • The Alberti bass — broken chords in the left hand — was a common piano pattern.
  • Famous composers: Haydn, Mozart, and the young Beethoven.

Step 5

Characteristics of Sonata Form

Znaky sonátovej formy – obdobie klasicizmu

  • Composers used sonata form for the first part of symphonies, sonatas, string quartets, and concertos.
  • It has three parts: Exposition → Development → Recapitulation (and, sometimes, an introduction and a coda too).
  • Exposition: we hear the first theme in the home key (tonic). Then a bridge. Then a second theme in a different key.
  • Development: the themes change. The music moves through different keys. This makes tension.
  • Recapitulation: the themes come back. Now both themes are in the home key (tonic). The tension is gone.
  • The first theme is often strong. The second theme is often soft. This contrast is easy to hear.

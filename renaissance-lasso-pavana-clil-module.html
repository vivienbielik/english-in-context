2Learn

The Renaissance was a period in Europe from about 1400 to 1600. People became very interested in art, science, and beauty. In music, composers wrote many voices singing together. This style is called polyphony — "poly" means many, "phony" means sound.

🎼 Who was Orlando di Lasso?

Orlando di Lasso (about 1532–1594) was a composer from Flanders (today Belgium). He worked for many years at the royal court in Munich, Germany. He wrote more than 2,000 pieces of music — masses, motets, madrigals, and funny songs.

💃 What is a pavane?

A pavane (pavana) was a slow, elegant dance for the royal court. Dancers walked in pairs, in a stately, gliding step. It was often followed by a faster dance called the galliard.

Timeline: Life of Orlando di Lasso

c. 1532 — Born in Mons, in the Spanish Netherlands (now Belgium).
as a boy — He sang beautifully in the church choir. People say he was "kidnapped" three times because other choirs wanted his voice!
1544–1554 — He travelled and worked in Italy, learning the Italian style of music.
1556 — He moved to Munich and became the court composer for the Duke of Bavaria.
1594 — He died in Munich, one of the most famous composers of his time.

🕺 Renaissance court dances

At royal parties, musicians played on instruments like the lute, recorder, viol, and crumhorn. Nobles danced the slow pavane, then the fast galliard. The pavane's name may come from the word "peacock" — dancers moved with a proud, elegant walk, like a peacock showing its feathers!

4Listen

🎵 Extract 1 — "Matona mia cara" (Orlando di Lasso)
Listen and think:
  1. How many singers can you hear? Is it one voice or many voices together?
  2. Is the music fast or slow? Happy or sad?
  3. Do the voices sing the same words at the same time, or do they "answer" each other?
🎵 Extract 2 — "The Battle Pavane" (Tielman Susato)
Listen and think:
  1. Can you hear a steady walking rhythm? Try to tap it with your hand.
  2. What instruments can you hear — strings, brass, or wind instruments?
  3. Close your eyes. Can you imagine dancers walking slowly across a castle hall?

English Language Teaching › B2 Level English – Grammar › Revision of Present and Past Tenses Grammar 1 Revision of Present and Past Tenses Six tenses carry most of what you say and write at B2 level – two present, two past, and the perfect forms that connect them. The choice between them is rarely about grammar rules alone; it’s about the speaker’s point of view – is the action finished, ongoing, or does it link to another moment in time? 1. Present Simple vs. Present Continuous Present Simple describes facts, routines, permanent situations, and timetables – things that are true in general, not just right now. She teaches at the secondary grammar school. · The museum opens at nine. Present Continuous describes actions happening at the moment of speaking, temporary situations, and changing or developing trends. I ’m writing a new worksheet right now. · More schools are introducing CLIL this year. always · usually · every day · on Mondays now · at the moment · currently · these days Note: verbs describing states rather than actions (know, believe, want, seem, belong) are rarely used in the continuous form. Quick practice – Present Simple vs. Present Continuous 1. My colleagues usually (mark) exams by hand, but this term they (use) an online system. 2. Look! The Erasmus+ coordinator (upload) the new photos to the website right now. 3. Our school (organise) an international project every two years. Check answers Show answers Clear 2. Past Simple vs. Past Continuous Past Simple describes completed actions or a sequence of events in the past. She visited Vienna last spring. · He finished the report before lunch. Past Continuous describes an action in progress at a specific past moment, often as background to another event. I was preparing the lesson when the power went out. · While she was marking tests, the phone rang. yesterday · last year · in 2015 · ago while · when · at 8 o’clock yesterday Note: the two forms often work together – the continuous sets the scene, the simple interrupts it. Quick practice – Past Simple vs. Past Continuous 1. While the students (rehearse) for the concert, the fire alarm (go) off. 2. I (walk) home when I (see) my old teacher. 3. At eight o’clock last night, my grandmother (watch) the news, as she always did. Check answers Show answers Clear 3. Present Perfect Simple vs. Past Simple Present Perfect connects the past to now – a result that matters today, an unfinished period, or an unspecified time. I ’ve already sent the report. · She has taught here for over thirty years. Past Simple is used instead whenever the sentence names a specific, finished point in the past. I sent the report yesterday. · She started teaching in 1994. already · just · yet · ever · never · since · for · so far yesterday · in 2020 · when I was at school Note: a stated past time word (yesterday, in 2020…) always rules out the Present Perfect. Quick practice – Present Perfect Simple vs. Past Simple 1. We (already / send) the permission forms to the parents. 2. Erasmus+ (change) the way our school works since we joined in 2013. 3. She (graduate) from university in 2010. Check answers Show answers Clear 4. Present Perfect Continuous This form highlights the duration of an activity that began in the past and continues now – or has just stopped, leaving a visible result. I ’ve been correcting essays since seven o’clock. · Her eyes are red – she ’s been crying. for · since · all day · recently · lately Note: compare with Present Perfect Simple, which focuses on the completed result rather than the duration – I’ve been reading that book (still going) vs. I’ve read that book (finished). Quick practice – Present Perfect Continuous 1. The IT technician (work) on the new website all afternoon. 2. How long you (learn) English? 3. My eyes hurt because I (mark) essays for the last three hours. Check answers Show answers Clear 5. Past Perfect The “past before the past” – an action completed before another past action or point in time. When I arrived, the lesson had already started. · She hadn’t finished the exercise before the bell rang. already · just · before · after · by the time · never Note: Past Perfect is usually paired with Past Simple to make the order of two past events unambiguous. Quick practice – Past Perfect 1. By the time the inspector arrived, we (already / prepare) all the documents. 2. She was upset because she (not / receive) the results yet. 3. When the bell rang, the teacher (just / finish) explaining the homework. Past Perfect already, just, before, after, by the time, never, when (+ two past events) Memory hack Happening NOW → simple/continuous present. FINISHED at a stated time → past simple.

2Reading

Text A — A Voice for Change

B2/C1 · Biography

Malala Yousafzai grew up in the Swat Valley in Pakistan, at a time when a local extremist movement was closing girls' schools in the region. Rather than staying silent, she began writing about her experiences and speaking publicly about a girl's right to education. In 2012, when she was just fifteen, a gunman shot her on her way home from school because of her activism. She survived, and instead of retreating from public life, she became an even more determined campaigner, whose message reached a global audience.

In 2014, Malala became the youngest person ever to receive the Nobel Peace Prize, an honour she shared with Indian activist Kailash Satyarthi. She has since co-founded a fund that supports education projects for girls in several countries. Supporters describe her as resilient, principled and remarkably humble for someone with such an extraordinary public profile — she rarely presents herself as a hero, insisting instead that the real work is done by ordinary girls and teachers whose stories are less well known.

Glossary: extremist movement — a group with very strong, often violent political or religious views; campaigner — a person who works to achieve social or political change; profile — the level of public attention someone receives.

True, False, or Not Given?

1. Malala stopped speaking publicly about education after the attack.
2. Malala shared the Nobel Peace Prize with another activist.
3. Malala plans to run for political office in the future.
4. According to the text, Malala avoids presenting herself as a hero.

Text B — The Rise and Fall of a Social Media Idol

B2/C1 · Feature article (fictional case study)

Zac Mercer was, for a while, one of the most followed lifestyle influencers online. He built his image around confidence and success, whose secret — he claimed — was a strict daily routine that "anyone could follow." Fans who bought his courses and merchandise treated him almost like a guru. Behind the curated photos, however, was a very different story. Former employees whom journalists later interviewed described a manipulative boss who took credit for other people's ideas and quietly dropped anyone who stopped being useful to him.

The turning point came when a documentary revealed that several of the "transformation" stories he had promoted were staged, and that the investors whose money he had used to expand his business had never actually seen a return. Mercer, whose online persona had always seemed so genuine, was suddenly accused of being deceitful, vain and, according to one former business partner, entirely self-centred. His following collapsed within weeks. Commentators used the story as a warning: an impressive online image is not the same thing as good character.

Glossary: curated — carefully selected and presented; guru — a person other people go to for advice, often on lifestyle or self-improvement; persona — the public image or character someone presents to others.

Multiple choice

1. What eventually caused Zac Mercer's downfall?
He was sued by a fan for false advertising.
A documentary exposed that some of his claims were staged.
He retired from social media voluntarily.
2. According to former employees, how did Mercer behave towards people who were no longer useful to him?
He continued to support them out of loyalty.
He publicly apologised to them.
He quietly stopped associating with them.
3. What is the main message of the text?
A polished public image doesn't guarantee good character.
All influencers are dishonest.
Documentaries are an unreliable source of information.

4Grammar Lab — Relative Clauses

We often use relative clauses to describe the people we admire (or don't). Read the rules below carefully.

1. Defining relative clauses

Give essential information — without them, the sentence loses its meaning. No commas.

PronounUsed forExample
whopeople (subject)A role model is someone who inspires others to be better.
whompeople (object, formal)The mentor whom I most admire taught me for two years.
whosepossessionMalala, whose courage is well known, won the Nobel Prize.
which/thatthingsThe charity which/that she founded supports girls' education.

2. Non-defining relative clauses

Add extra, non-essential information. Always separated by commas. "That" cannot be used here.

Zac Mercer, who once had millions of followers, lost his reputation almost overnight.

His business partners, whose money had disappeared, demanded an investigation.

⚠ Common mistakes

✗ The woman which I admire is a scientist. → ✓ The woman who/that I admire is a scientist. (who/that for people, not which)

✗ Malala, that survived an attack, became a global symbol. → ✓ Malala, who survived an attack, became a global symbol. (no "that" in non-defining clauses)

6Discuss & Debate

In pairs or small groups, choose one prompt and discuss for 5–10 minutes. Try to use at least two relative clauses and three character-trait words from this unit.

🎤

Idol vs. role model

Can someone be a great idol (famous, talented) but a poor role model (bad character)? Give real or invented examples.

📱

Social media influence

Are today's influencers held to a lower standard than traditional public figures like teachers or scientists? Why might that be?

⚖️

Second chances

If a role model is later revealed to have negative traits, should we forgive them if they change? Debate both sides.

👪

Everyday heroes

Must a role model be famous, or can someone in your own life (a parent, teacher, coach) be just as important? Explain with examples.

7Create & Reflect

Write a short paragraph (100–150 words) about a real person who is a role model for you. Try to include at least two relative clauses and three character-trait adjectives from this unit.

🗣️

Share aloud

Read your paragraph to a partner. Can they identify which traits you used?

📰

Write a mini profile

Turn your paragraph into a short magazine-style profile with a headline.

🎭

Interview role-play

In pairs, interview each other "as" your role models, answering in character.

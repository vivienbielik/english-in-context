📚 Grammar Review: Telling a Life Story in the Past

A biography moves along a line of events told in the Past Simple. Whenever the story needs to step back to something even earlier, we switch to the Past Perfect: Simple for completed actions and results, Continuous for activities in progress and their duration.

Past Simple Past Perfect Simple Past Perfect Continuous

Past Simple: the main line of the story

verb + -ed / irregular form (took, won, led) · did + infinitive in questions and negatives
  • Completed actions at a definite time: "Kennedy became president in 1961."
  • A sequence of events in the order they happened: "Mandela walked out of prison, raised his fist and smiled at the crowd."
  • Past states and habits: "Churchill painted to relax."
  • Typical words: in 1940, ago, last year, then, when, after that, on 11 February.

Past Perfect Simple: the earlier, completed action

had + past participle (had won, had led, hadn't expected)
had served in the Navy → entered politics
  • An action completed before another past action or time: "Kennedy had served in the Navy before he entered politics."
  • Results and numbers by a past moment (how many / how much): "By 1990, Mandela had spent 27 years in prison."
  • Stative verbs (be, know, believe, own) even with for + time: "Britain had been at war for eight months."
  • Typical words: by the time, by 1960, before, after, already, just, never … until, once.

Past Perfect Continuous: the earlier activity and its duration

had been + -ing (had been campaigning, hadn't been sleeping)
had been warning about Hitler for years → the war began
  • An activity that lasted up to a past moment (how long, for, since): "Churchill had been warning about Hitler for years when the war began."
  • The cause of a past situation or a visible result: "The prisoners' eyes were damaged because they had been working in a bright limestone quarry."
  • An activity that was still in progress or unfinished: "The Soviets had been building missile bases when the Americans spotted them."

C1 patterns that examiners love

  • It was the first / second / only time + Past Perfect: "It was the first time Mandela had ever voted."
  • No sooner had … than / Hardly (Scarcely) had … when (inversion): "No sooner had Kennedy taken office than he faced his first crisis."
  • Unfulfilled hopes and plans with had hoped / had intended / had meant to: "Kennedy had hoped to serve two terms."
  • Time since: "Six years had passed since Churchill had led Britain to victory."
  • Reported speech backshift: "He said he had never given up."

💡 Tips for Slovak speakers

  • Slovak has one past tense for all three meanings (Slúžil v námorníctve, kým vstúpil do politiky). In English, ask: Which happened first? The earlier action goes into the Past Perfect.
  • Don't overuse the Past Perfect. Once you have stepped back in time, you can return to the Past Simple. And with before or after, the Past Simple is often enough because the order is already clear.
  • With for + time, choose the Continuous for activities (had been campaigning) and the Simple for states (had been in prison, had known).

🏛️ Model Statements: Three Leaders, Three Life Stories

Read the model statements and notice how the colours change. The Past Simple carries the story forward; the Past Perfect steps back to explain what had happened before.

Past Simple Past Perfect Simple Past Perfect Continuous

John F. Kennedy

1917 – 1963 · 35th President of the USA
WWII naval heroPulitzer Prize 1957Peace CorpsCuban Missile Crisis
  1. Before he entered politics, Kennedy had served as a naval officer in the Second World War.
  2. He wrote Profiles in Courage while he was recovering from back surgery, and the book won the Pulitzer Prize in 1957.
  3. He had been campaigning for almost a year when he narrowly won the 1960 election.
  4. When the Americans discovered the missile bases in Cuba, the Soviets had been building them for weeks.
  5. By the time he was assassinated in 1963, he had signed the first treaty limiting nuclear tests.

Nelson Mandela

1918 – 2013 · First President of democratic South Africa
Lawyer27 years in prisonNobel Peace Prize 1993President 1994–1999
  1. By the time he opened the country's first Black-owned law firm in 1952, he had already joined the ANC.
  2. In 1964, the court sentenced him to life imprisonment.
  3. His eyes were damaged because he had been working in a bright limestone quarry on Robben Island.
  4. When he walked free in 1990, he had spent 27 years behind bars.
  5. When he voted in 1994, it was the first time he had ever voted in his life.

Winston Churchill

1874 – 1965 · British Prime Minister 1940–45, 1951–55
War correspondentWartime leader"Iron Curtain" speechNobel Prize in Literature 1953
  1. As a young war correspondent in 1899, he escaped from a prisoner-of-war camp in South Africa.
  2. In 1915, he lost his post at the Admiralty after the disastrous Gallipoli campaign.
  3. When the war broke out in 1939, he had been warning about the danger of Nazi Germany for years.
  4. He became Prime Minister in May 1940, when Britain had been at war for eight months.
  5. By the time he received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1953, he had written dozens of books.
Why is it "had been at war" and not "had been warring"? Because be at war describes a state. States take the Past Perfect Simple, even with for + time. Look out for this trap in Exercise 1.

Model answer: Nelson Mandela

Nelson Mandela was a lawyer who became the symbol of the fight against apartheid. By the time he was arrested in the early 1960s, he had been campaigning for equal rights for almost twenty years.

The turning point came in 1990. When he finally walked free, he had spent 27 years in prison, and crowds had been waiting for hours to see him. Remarkably, he had not lost his belief in reconciliation. No sooner had he left prison than he began talks with the government.

In 1994, it was the first time he had ever voted, and he became president. For me, he showed that forgiveness is a form of strength.

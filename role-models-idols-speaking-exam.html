Role Models and Idols

Prepare a 5-minute individual presentation. Don't simply describe a famous person: reflect on the wider importance and influence of role models in society.

See the task Practise with the timer
⏱ approx. 5 minutes 🎤 individual presentation B2–C1

Task

Prepare a 5-minute individual presentation on the topic ROLE MODELS AND IDOLS.

Your presentation should be well organised, fluent, and supported by relevant examples. You should not simply describe a famous person; you should also reflect on the wider importance and influence of role models in society.

What to talk about

In your presentation, address the following points.

1

Define the terms

  • What is a role model?
  • What is an idol?
  • Is there a difference between the two?
2

Personal perspective

  • Who do you consider to be a role model or an inspiring person?
  • What qualities, achievements, or values make this person admirable?
  • In what way has this person influenced you or others?
3

Role models in society

  • Why do people, especially young people, need role models?
  • Where do we usually find them: family, school, sport, culture, politics, social media, etc.?
  • What qualities should a good role model have?
4

Celebrity culture and social media

  • Why do people idolise celebrities, influencers, athletes, musicians, or public figures?
  • Can social media create unrealistic or unhealthy idols?
  • Should famous people be expected to behave as role models?
5

Critical reflection

  • Can admiring someone too much become dangerous?
  • What happens when a role model disappoints us or behaves controversially?
  • Is it better to admire one person or to take inspiration from several different people?

Conclusion

Finish your presentation by answering this question:

Final question
What makes someone truly worthy of admiration?

Language expectations

Try to use:

Advanced vocabulary

connected with personality, influence, success, values and society

Linking expressions

a range of linking expressions

Clear arguments

supported by examples

Complex grammar

complex grammatical structures

Opinions

appropriate expressions for giving and justifying opinions

Useful expressions

Build these into your presentation.

I would distinguish between … and …
What I find particularly admirable is …
One of the main reasons I look up to this person is …
This person has had a significant influence on …
It could be argued that …
On the one hand …, whereas on the other hand …
A potential drawback of idolising celebrities is …
From my perspective …
Ultimately, I believe that …

Important

⚠️

Do not read a prepared text word for word. You may use brief notes or keywords, but you should speak naturally and maintain eye contact.

Speaking time: approximately 5 minutes.

Practise your timing

Rehearse out loud and aim for about five minutes.

🪞 Vocabulary Focus: Words with self-

Many words that describe character begin with self-. They are useful for talking about role models, both positively and negatively.

Three patterns

self- + adjective · self- + past participle · self- + noun
self- + adjective: self-confident, self-conscious, self-sufficient
self- + past participle: self-made, self-taught, self-absorbed, self-centred
self- + noun: self-esteem, self-doubt, self-discipline, self-sacrifice

Spelling and pronunciation

always with a hyphen: self-made ✓ selfmade ✗
The main stress is usually on the second part: self-CONfident, self-MADE, self-esTEEM. Many adjectives have a matching noun: self-confident → self-confidence, self-disciplined → self-discipline.
Positive 👍Negative 👎
self-confident, self-assuredself-centred, self-absorbed
self-disciplined, self-motivatedself-conscious (insecure)
self-reliant, self-sufficient, self-taughtself-doubt
self-effacing, self-sacrificeself-promotion (often negative)

⚠️ Watch out, Slovak speakers!

  • self-conscious ≠ sebavedomý. It means neistý, rozpačitý: worried about how others see you. "I feel self-conscious when I speak in public."
  • sebavedomý = self-confident. "She is self-confident on stage."
  • self-esteem is about how you value yourself inside (sebaúcta); self-confidence is about trust in your abilities.

⏪ Grammar Focus: Past Perfect Simple & Continuous

When we tell the story of a role model's life, we often need to go back to an even earlier past: what had happened, or what had been happening, before a key moment.

Past Perfect Simple: the earlier, completed action

had + past participle (had won, had written, hadn't known)
had released three albums → became famous
  • An action completed before another past action or time: "She had released three albums before she became famous."
  • Results and numbers (how many / how much): "By 25, he had won four world titles."
  • With stative verbs (know, believe, like, own): "I had known him for years."
  • Typical words: by the time, before, after, when, already, just, never … until, by (2010).

Past Perfect Continuous: the earlier activity and its duration

had been + -ing (had been training, hadn't been sleeping)
had been training for 12 years → won gold
  • An activity that lasted up to a past moment (how long, for, since): "She had been training for twelve years when she finally won gold."
  • The cause of a past situation or a visible result: "He was exhausted because he had been touring for months."
  • An activity that may be unfinished: "They had been working on the film when the scandal broke."

Focusresult, completionduration, process
QuestionHow many? How much?How long?
ExampleShe had written five songs.She had been writing songs all day.
Stative verbs✓ had known, had believed✗ had been knowing

💡 Tip for Slovak speakers

  • Slovak uses the ordinary past tense for both actions (Vydala tri albumy, kým sa stala slávnou). In English, ask yourself: Which happened first? That earlier action goes into the Past Perfect.
  • If the order is already clear from before or after, the Past Simple is sometimes possible too: "She released three albums before she became famous."
  • Don't overuse it: if you are simply telling events in order, use the Past Simple.

🗣️ Speaking: Before They Were Famous

Choose a role model, famous or someone from your own life, and tell their story. Use at least three Past Perfect forms and five words from the cards.

⏪ The road to success

  • What had this person done before they became successful?
  • How long had they been working towards their goal?
  • What obstacles had they overcome?

🪞 Character

  • Is this person self-made, self-disciplined or self-effacing?
  • Have they ever seemed self-centred or overrated?
  • What qualities have made them a role model rather than just an idol?

💥 A turning point

  • Describe the moment that changed their life.
  • What had been happening just before it?
  • How did people react?

🌱 You

  • By the time you were 15, what had you already achieved?
  • What had you been dreaming about as a child?
  • What legacy would you like to leave?

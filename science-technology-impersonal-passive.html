Part 1 · Grammar

Language Lab: The Impersonal Passive

"It is believed that…", "The drug is thought to have been…": reporting structures are everywhere in science news, medical reports and technology articles. They let the writer report opinions and facts without saying who holds them.

Two patterns, one meaning

Active: Scientists believe that the new vaccine protects against all variants.

Pattern A (It + passive + that-clause): It is believed that the new vaccine protects against all variants.

Pattern B (Subject + passive + to-infinitive): The new vaccine is believed to protect against all variants.

Pattern B is shorter and very common in formal writing. The subject of the that-clause (the new vaccine) becomes the subject of the whole sentence.

1. Verbs used in impersonal passives

Both patterns (It… / Subject…)Only Pattern A (It… that)
say, think, believe, know, consider, expect, report, claim, estimate, understand, allege, assume, fear, suppose, find, acknowledgehope, suggest, propose, recommend, agree, decide, argue, announce
The robot is said to work 24 hours a day.It is hoped that the treatment will be cheaper.
✗ The treatment is hoped to be cheaper.

2. The key rule: choose the right infinitive

Compare two moments: the time of the reporting verb (is said, was believed) and the time of the action you are reporting.

Relation to the reporting verbInfinitiveExample
Same time / general truthto + verbThe scanner is believed to detect tumours earlier.
Same time, happening nowto be + -ingEngineers are reported to be testing a new maglev train.
Same time, passiveto be + past participleThe drug is expected to be approved next year.
Earlier actionto have + past participlePenicillin is estimated to have saved over 200 million lives.
Earlier action, lastingto have been + -ingThe hackers are thought to have been operating for months.
Earlier action, passiveto have been + past participleThe crash is thought to have been caused by a software error.

Future: with expect (and often believe, think), a simple infinitive can refer to the future: Air taxis are expected to start commercial flights soon. With other verbs, use Pattern A with will: It is believed that 6G will be introduced around 2030.

3. Step by step: from active to impersonal

StepExample
1. Active sentenceExperts think that the satellite was damaged by space debris.
2. Pattern A: It + be + past participle + thatIt is thought that the satellite was damaged by space debris.
3. Move the subject of the that-clause to the frontThe satellite is thought…
4. Earlier and passive → to have been + PPThe satellite is thought to have been damaged by space debris.

If the reporting verb is in the past, keep it in the past: People believed that the drug was safe → The drug was believed to be safe. (Both happened at the same time, so we use the simple infinitive.)

4. There + impersonal passive

It is thought that there is a link between screen time and poor sleep.
→ There is thought to be a link between screen time and poor sleep.
It is said that there were problems with the first prototype.
→ There are said to have been problems with the first prototype.

Part 2 · Exam Hacks

Exam Hacks: Get It Right Every Time

Quick rules to remember before a test, a keyword transformation task or the maturita exam.

💡 Hack 1: Two clocks. Always ask: did the action happen at the same time as the reporting, or before it? Same time → to do / to be doing / to be done. Before → to have done / to have been doing / to have been done.
💡 Hack 2: Copy the verb from the that-clause. The verb in the that-clause tells you which infinitive to use: is testing → to be testing, has saved → to have saved, was caused → to have been caused, had been working → to have been working.
💡 Hack 3: Don't drop "be". The reporting verb itself must be passive: The phone is said to…, not The phone says to…. This is the most common mistake in tests.
💡 Hack 4: "Hope", "suggest", "recommend" only work with "It". It is hoped that… ✓ / The cure is hoped to… ✗
💡 Hack 5: Intransitive verbs stay active in the infinitive. The virus is believed to have originated in bats, not to have been originated: "originate", "happen" and "occur" have no passive.
💡 Hack 6: Word-count transformations. In keyword tasks (3–6 words), contractions count as two words, and the key word must not be changed. Structures like is thought to have been use up many words, so check your count.
💡 Hack 7: "Be supposed to" has two meanings. You are supposed to wear a mask = you should. The app is supposed to be very secure = people say it is.
🇸🇰 For Slovak speakers: Slovak often uses vraj, údajne or predpokladá sa, že…. For example, Údajne ho vyvinuli v Japonsku = It is said to have been developed in Japan. Don't translate word for word: ✗ It is said him to have…

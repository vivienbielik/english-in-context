💡 Watch out: play, go or do?

  • play + ball games and competitive games: play football, tennis, chess.
  • go + activities ending in -ing: go swimming, skiing, cycling, climbing.
  • do + individual activities, martial arts and athletics: do yoga, judo, athletics, aerobics, gymnastics.
  • win a match / a medal / a race · beat a person or team: We beat Poland. (never We won Poland ❌)
  • score a goal / points · gain an advantage · earn respect.
  • a referee (football, hockey, basketball) · an umpire (tennis, cricket, baseball) · a judge (gymnastics, figure skating).

📰 Reading: The Fastest Man Who Never Was

A fictional doping story full of speculation. Notice how the speakers show how sure they are – you will analyse it in Exercise 7.

1When the 23-year-old cyclist Marco Delvecchio crossed the finish line four minutes ahead of everyone else, the crowd went wild. The experts, however, were silent. "That can't be natural," one former champion said on live television. "Nobody climbs a mountain that fast. He must be taking something."

2Two weeks later, the results of his drug test were published. Delvecchio had tested positive for a banned substance that increases the number of red blood cells and gives athletes extra stamina. He denied all the allegations. "Somebody must have put it in my drink," he told journalists. "I might have eaten contaminated meat. I have never cheated in my life."

3The anti-doping agency was not convinced. The substance could not have entered his body by accident, its scientists explained, because it has to be injected. Delvecchio was banned for four years and stripped of his title. His team manager admitted that the warning signs had been there. "We should have asked more questions when his results improved so suddenly," he said.

4Cases like this damage more than one career. Clean athletes who finished behind Delvecchio may have lost sponsorship deals and prize money, and young fans who saw him as a role model must have felt betrayed. As one teammate put it: "He needn't have done it. He was talented enough to win without drugs."

5Some people argue that doping is impossible to stop, so it should simply be legalised and controlled by doctors. Most experts disagree. A level playing field, they say, is the whole point of sport. If we allow drugs, young athletes will feel they have no choice but to risk their health, and the values of fair play and sportsmanship might disappear completely.

🔍 Modal Verbs of Deduction and Speculation

Detectives, sports commentators and Maturita candidates all need to guess logically. The modal shows how sure you are.

How sure?ModalExample
✅ 100% sure it's true (logical conclusion)must + infinitiveShe trains six hours a day. She must be exhausted.
🤔 possible (maybe 50%)might / may / could + infinitiveHe's not at training. He might be injured.
🤔 possible but less likelymight not / may not + infinitiveThe coach may not know about it yet.
❌ 100% sure it's NOT truecan't / couldn't + infinitiveHe can't be the goalkeeper – he's only 1.60 m tall!
📈 expectationshould / ought to + infinitiveThe match started at 6, so it should be over by now.

🔄 Continuous: what is happening right now?

must / might / can't + be + -ing
The stadium is so loud – Slovakia must be winning.
She isn't answering. She might be training.

🚫 The negative of must in deduction is can't

must ↔ can't (deduction) · must ↔ mustn't (obligation)
He can't be tired – he's only run 100 metres. ✅
He mustn't be tired. ❌ (mustn't = it is forbidden)

❓ Questions

Could it be…? · Can it be true? · Might he…? (formal)
Could she be the new captain? · Can it really be true? – never May she be…? in this meaning.

⚠️ Typical mistakes

  • He must to be tired. → He must be tired. (modal + bare infinitive)
  • It mustn't be true! → It can't be true!
  • Maybe she is injured. → fine, but more advanced: She may / might / could be injured.
  • He can be at the gym now. → He could / might be at the gym now. (can = general possibility, not a guess about now)

⏪ Modal Verbs in the Past

Use modal + have + past participle to guess about the past, criticise, or talk about things that didn't happen.

Modal + have + past participleMeaningExample
must have doneI'm sure it happenedHe must have trained very hard to win.
can't / couldn't have doneI'm sure it didn't happenShe can't have seen the foul – she was looking the other way.
might / may / could have doneperhaps it happenedThe ball might have crossed the line.
might not / may not have doneperhaps it didn't happenHe may not have heard the whistle.
should / ought to have doneit was right, but it didn't happen (criticism, regret)You should have warmed up before running.
shouldn't have doneit was wrong, but it happenedHe shouldn't have argued with the referee.
could have doneit was possible, but it didn't happenWe could have won if we'd scored that penalty.
needn't have doneit happened, but it was unnecessaryI needn't have brought my racket – they had spare ones.
would have donean imagined past result (3rd conditional)I would have gone to the match, but I was ill.

🔄 Past continuous form

must / might / can't + have been + -ing
Her eyes were red. She must have been crying. · He can't have been paying attention.

⚖️ needn't have done vs didn't need to do

needn't have + 3rd form = you DID it (unnecessarily) · didn't need to + inf. = usually you DIDN'T do it
I needn't have taken a taxi – the stadium was close. (I took one.)
I didn't need to take a taxi – my dad drove me. (I didn't take one.)

⚖️ could have vs was able to

We could have won. = it was possible, but we didn't win.
We were able to win despite the injury. = we managed to win.

⚠️ Typical mistakes

  • He must have win. → He must have won. (past participle!)
  • She must had trained. → She must have trained.
  • He mustn't have seen it. → He can't have seen it.
  • You should of told me. → You should have told me. (should've sounds like "should of")

Discussion Lab

Three hot topics in sport

Work in pairs or small groups. Choose one question, add your own arguments and reach a conclusion together.

🎮 Should e-sports be in the Olympics?

+ Millions of fans, skill, strategy and fast reactions

− No physical effort, risks of a sedentary lifestyle

+ Attracts young viewers to the Games

− Games belong to private companies

💊 Should doping be legalised and controlled?

+ Ends hypocrisy; doctors could control the doses

− Health risks; young athletes would feel forced

+ Saves money spent on testing

− Destroys the level playing field and fair play

🏫 Should schools have PE every day?

+ Fights obesity, improves concentration

− Less time for academic subjects

+ Teaches team spirit and resilience

− Some students hate competitive sport

🗨️ Useful phrases

  • Speculating: It must be… · He can't have known… · They might have… · I'd say it could be…
  • Describing a photo: They must be celebrating – look at their faces. · She might be training for a race. · It can't be summer – there's snow.
  • Criticising past actions: The organisers should have… · He shouldn't have… · They could have…
Speaking Exam · Questions

Answer in 1–2 minutes each

Sport and you

  • What sports do you do and why?
  • How has sport shaped your personality?
  • Are you a good loser?

Types of sport

  • Do you prefer team or individual sports?
  • Which winter sports are popular in Slovakia?
  • Which sport would you never try, and why?

New trends

  • Why are extreme sports so popular?
  • Is e-sport a real sport?
  • Do fitness trackers help or put pressure on people?

Fair play

  • Why do some athletes cheat?
  • How should doping be punished?
  • Are top athletes paid too much?

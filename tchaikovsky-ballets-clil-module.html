Step 2 · Background

Tchaikovsky and the Ballet

Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky (1840–1893) was a Russian composer of the Romantic period. Before him, ballet music was often seen as simple background music for dancers. Tchaikovsky changed that: he wrote ballet scores with rich orchestral colours, memorable melodies and leitmotifs, short musical themes that belong to a character or an idea, just like in an opera or a film.

He composed only three ballets, but all three are still among the most performed ballets in the world. Two of them were created with the great French-Russian choreographer Marius Petipa at the Mariinsky Theatre in St Petersburg.

A ballet tells a story without words. The dancers use steps, gestures and mime to show what the characters feel, and the music tells us what is happening.

1877 · Swan Lake

His first ballet, premiered at the Bolshoi Theatre in Moscow. It was not a success at first.

1890 · The Sleeping Beauty

Created with Marius Petipa at the Mariinsky Theatre. Tchaikovsky himself was very proud of it.

1892 · The Nutcracker

His last ballet, premiered at the Mariinsky just before Christmas. Today it is a Christmas tradition all over the world.

The Three Stories

Choose a ballet. Read the story act by act, then listen to the famous music from each part.

Swan Lake

A love story about magic, deception and loyalty, in four acts.

Prince Siegfried · a young prince Odette · the White Swan, a princess under a spell Von Rothbart · an evil sorcerer Odile · the Black Swan, Rothbart's daughter The Queen · Siegfried's mother

Key facts

Music
P. I. Tchaikovsky
Premiere
1877, Bolshoi Theatre, Moscow
Famous version
1895, Marius Petipa & Lev Ivanov, St Petersburg
Acts
4

Act 1The prince must choose a bride

It is Prince Siegfried's birthday and there is a big celebration in the palace garden. His mother, the Queen, tells him that he is now an adult and must choose a wife at a grand ball the next evening. Siegfried is sad because he wants to marry for love. When evening comes, he sees a group of swans flying over and goes hunting with his crossbow.

Act 2Odette and the magic lake

At a lake in the forest, Siegfried is about to shoot a swan when it turns into a beautiful young woman: Odette, the Swan Queen. She explains that the evil sorcerer Von Rothbart has put a spell on her and her friends: they are swans by day and become human only at night. The lake is made of her mother's tears. The spell can only be broken if a man who has never loved before promises to love her forever. Siegfried falls in love with her and swears to be faithful. At dawn, Odette must turn back into a swan.

Act 3The ball and the Black Swan

At the ball, princesses from many countries dance for Siegfried, but he refuses them all. Suddenly Von Rothbart arrives in disguise with his daughter Odile, the Black Swan, who has been magically made to look exactly like Odette. Siegfried is completely deceived. He dances with Odile and announces that he will marry her. At that moment he sees the real Odette at the window, desperate, and realises that he has broken his promise.

Act 4Back at the lake

Siegfried runs to the lake and begs Odette to forgive him. She forgives him, but the spell can no longer be broken. In the best-known ending, Odette and Siegfried choose to throw themselves into the lake rather than live apart. Their love destroys Rothbart's power, and the lovers are united after death. Some productions have a happy ending instead, in which love defeats the sorcerer.

Did you know? Odette (the White Swan) and Odile (the Black Swan) are usually danced by the same ballerina. In Act 3, Odile performs 32 fouettés: fast turns on one leg, without stopping.

The Nutcracker

A magical Christmas adventure, in two acts.

Clara · a young girl (called Marie in some versions) Fritz · her naughty brother Drosselmeyer · Clara's mysterious godfather, a toymaker The Nutcracker · a wooden toy who becomes a prince The Mouse King · leader of the mice The Sugar Plum Fairy · ruler of the Land of Sweets

Key facts

Music
P. I. Tchaikovsky
Premiere
December 1892, Mariinsky Theatre, St Petersburg
Choreography
Marius Petipa & Lev Ivanov
Based on
E. T. A. Hoffmann, The Nutcracker and the Mouse King
Acts
2

Act 1 · Scene 1The Christmas Eve party

It is Christmas Eve at the Stahlbaum family's house. Friends and children arrive, and everyone dances around a beautiful Christmas tree. Clara's godfather, Drosselmeyer, arrives with magical mechanical dolls and gives Clara a special present: a wooden nutcracker shaped like a soldier. Her jealous brother Fritz grabs it and breaks it, but Drosselmeyer repairs it with a handkerchief.

Act 1 · Scene 2The battle at midnight

At midnight, Clara comes back downstairs to check on her nutcracker. Strange things start to happen: the Christmas tree grows enormous, the toys come to life, and an army of mice led by the Mouse King attacks. The Nutcracker leads the toy soldiers into battle. When the Mouse King is about to win, Clara throws her slipper at him and saves the Nutcracker, who turns into a handsome prince.

Act 1 · Scene 3The Land of Snow

The prince takes Clara on a journey through a snowy forest, where snowflakes dance around them in a beautiful waltz.

Act 2The Land of Sweets

They arrive in the Land of Sweets, where the Sugar Plum Fairy welcomes them. The prince tells the story of the battle, and to celebrate, sweets from around the world dance for Clara: Spanish chocolate, Arabian coffee, Chinese tea, Russian candy canes, marzipan shepherdesses with reed flutes, and finally the Waltz of the Flowers. The Sugar Plum Fairy and her cavalier dance a grand pas de deux. At the end, Clara wakes up under the Christmas tree with her nutcracker in her arms. Was it all a dream?

Did you know? In the Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy, Tchaikovsky used a new instrument he had discovered in Paris: the celesta, a keyboard instrument that sounds like little bells. He kept it secret so that no other Russian composer could use it first.

The Sleeping Beauty

A fairy tale of good and evil, in a prologue and three acts.

Princess Aurora · the king's daughter The Lilac Fairy · a good fairy, Aurora's protector Carabosse · an evil fairy Prince Désiré · a prince who finds Aurora King Florestan & the Queen · Aurora's parents

Key facts

Music
P. I. Tchaikovsky
Premiere
January 1890, Mariinsky Theatre, St Petersburg
Choreography
Marius Petipa
Based on
Charles Perrault's fairy tale
Acts
Prologue + 3

PrologueThe christening and the curse

The King and Queen celebrate the christening of their baby daughter, Aurora. Six good fairies come to give her magical gifts such as beauty, kindness and grace. Suddenly the evil fairy Carabosse arrives, furious because she was not invited. She puts a curse on the baby: on her sixteenth birthday, Aurora will prick her finger on a spindle and die. The Lilac Fairy, who has not yet given her gift, cannot remove the curse, but she can make it softer: Aurora will not die; she will fall asleep for a hundred years, until a prince wakes her with a kiss.

Act 1Aurora's sixteenth birthday

The King has banned all spindles from the kingdom. On Aurora's sixteenth birthday, four princes come to ask for her hand, and she dances with each of them in the famous Rose Adagio. An old woman gives Aurora a strange present, a spindle. Aurora plays with it, pricks her finger and falls to the ground. The old woman throws off her cloak: it is Carabosse! The Lilac Fairy arrives and puts the whole palace to sleep, and a thick forest grows around the castle.

Act 2A hundred years later

Prince Désiré is hunting in the forest. The Lilac Fairy appears and shows him a vision of the beautiful sleeping princess, and he falls in love at once. She leads him through the forest to the castle, where he kisses Aurora. She wakes up, the spell is broken and the whole palace comes back to life.

Act 3The wedding

Aurora and Désiré celebrate their wedding. Characters from other fairy tales come to dance at the party: Puss in Boots and the White Cat, Little Red Riding Hood and the Wolf, and the Bluebird with Princess Florine. The ballet ends with a grand celebration.

Did you know? Disney used Tchaikovsky's famous waltz from Act 1 for the song Once Upon a Dream in its 1959 film Sleeping Beauty.

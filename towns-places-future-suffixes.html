💡 Watch out: easily confused words

  • a town (mesto) · a city (veľké mesto, veľkomesto) · a village (dedina). Levice is a town, Bratislava is a city.
  • in the centre but on the outskirts · in a suburb · on a housing estate · in the high street (BrE) / on Main Street (AmE).
  • renovate = modernise and repair · restore = bring back to its original look · convert into = change what it is used for.
  • historic = important in history (a historic building) · historical = connected with the past (a historical novel).
  • economic (hospodársky: economic growth) · economical (úsporný: an economical car).
  • a sight (pamätihodnosť: see the sights) · a site (miesto, lokalita: a building site) · a view (výhľad).

🗣️ Group Discussion Lab: What Does Our Town Need?

At a school meeting, students discuss ideas for an Innovative Community Competition: the local authority will fund the most popular idea. Learn the four groups of phrases, then practise them.

A · Introducing requirements

  • My main requirement is …
  • The first thing I'd look for is …
  • Above all, we need …

B · Essential requirements

  • For me, it's a must.
  • What we really need is …
  • We can't do without …
  • … is absolutely essential.

C · Desirable requirements

  • We could do with (a park).
  • I think that's a nice-to-have.
  • (A sports club) would be a big plus.
  • It would be great to have …, but it isn't essential.

D · Evaluating requirements

  • I'm not convinced by (the sports club).
  • I think the last idea is the best.
  • It has my vote.
  • That should be a priority.
  • It could have real long-term benefits.

The five ideas on the table

🎹 Musical steps

Stairs in the shopping centre that play notes like a piano when you walk on them.

+ fun, gets people to use the stairs

− a gimmick, little long-term value

🌱 Street party

Streets are closed, the road is covered with artificial grass, people have a picnic and play games.

+ cheap, simple, builds a sense of community

− one day only, traffic diversions

🎨 Underpass galleries

Dark underground pedestrian walkways become community art galleries.

+ safer, brighter, gives local artists space

− risk of vandalism, needs maintenance

🎪 Street circus

Performers bring underused or abandoned public spaces back to life.

+ puts neglected areas back on the map, encourages people to use them again

− noise, cost of performers

🏀 Sports club

A free club with courts and coaches for teenagers.

+ healthy, keeps young people off the streets

− expensive to run, needs a building

🔮 Grammar Focus: Talking about the Future

English has no single "future tense". The form you choose depends on how sure you are, when you decided and whether it is arranged.

FormUseExample (towns & places)
will + infinitivepredictions and opinions (I think, probably, I'm sure); instant decisions; offers, promisesI think our town will grow. · OK, I'll show you the way to the castle.
be going to + infinitiveplans and intentions decided before; predictions based on present evidenceThe council is going to build a skatepark. · Look at the traffic, we're going to be late.
present continuousfixed personal arrangements (time, place, people)We're meeting the mayor on Friday at 2.
present simpletimetables, schedules, programmesThe last bus leaves at 22.40. · The festival starts on 3 July.
might / may / couldpossibility, not certainThey might open a cinema in the old factory.
be about to / be on the point ofvery near futureHurry up, the concert is about to start.
be likely to / be bound to / be due toprobability / certainty / official planRents are bound to rise. · The bridge is due to reopen in May.
future continuous
will be + -ing		action in progress at a future timeWhen it's finished, the statue will be pointing towards the hills.
future perfect
will have + past participle		action completed before a future time (by)By 2035, the city will have doubled in size.
Adverbs with willPosition: after will, before won't.
It'll probably rain. It probably won't rain.
Certainty scalewill definitely → is bound to → will probably → is likely to → may / might → probably won't → definitely won't
Spontaneous vs planned– We're out of milk. – I'll get some. (now)
I'm going to get milk later. (planned)
Arrangement vs timetableI'm leaving on the 7.15 train. (my arrangement)
The train leaves at 7.15. (timetable)

⏱️ Grammar Focus: Using the Present to Talk about the Future

In subordinate clauses, English uses a present tense instead of will + infinitive, even though the meaning is future. Slovak uses the future here (keď prídem), so this is a classic Slovak mistake.

1 · After conjunctions of time

when · until / till · after · before · as soon as · once · by the time · the moment · while
When the memorial is finished, it will be ten times higher than Mount Rushmore.
As soon as the show starts, the lights will go down.
By the time we get home, it will be dark.

2 · In clauses of condition

if · unless · whether (… or not) · on condition that · as long as / so long as · provided (that) · supposing · in case
We won't be able to see the way down unless we leave soon.
If we stay any longer, we'll miss the film.
Supposing the monument isn't finished, will people forget about it?
Take a map in case you get lost.

3 · After relative pronouns and words like than and where

who · which · that · where · than · whatever · anyone who
Anyone who asks the locals about it will get different answers.
The square will be busier than it is now.
I'll go to any place where I can find a good job.

💡 Present simple or present perfect?

  • Use the present perfect to stress that the first action will be completed before the second: I'll call you when I've finished my homework.
  • Use the present continuous for an action in progress: Don't phone me while I'm driving.

⚠️ Exceptions: when will IS correct after when / if / whether

  • Indirect questions (the clause is the object of the verb): I don't know when the new pool will open. I wonder if it will rain.
  • if + will for willingness or polite requests: If you'll follow me, I'll show you the gallery.
  • if + will when the result comes first: I'll take an umbrella if it'll help. (rare, C1)

Typical mistakes

  • I'll text you when I will be at the station. → I'll text you when I'm at the station.
  • If it will be sunny, we'll go to the park. → If it's sunny, we'll go to the park.
  • We'll wait until the rain will stop. → We'll wait until the rain stops.
  • Whoever will win will get a prize. → Whoever wins will get a prize.
  • After I will finish school, I'm going to travel. → After I finish / have finished school, I'm going to travel.

🧩 Word Formation: Suffixes

A suffix changes the word class (verb → noun, noun → adjective …). In the Maturita word-formation task, first decide which word class the gap needs, then choose the suffix.

Insight: adjective suffixes -able and -ible

Both mean "can be done" or "having this quality": visible = can be seen, valuable = has value. They sound the same, so the spelling is the problem. These rules work in most cases:

✅ -able

  • The root is a complete word: acceptable, affordable, considerable, readable.
  • Complete word minus final -e: valuable, advisable, usable.
  • y → i: reliable, deniable.
  • Root ends in a hard c or g (/k/, /g/): amicable, navigable, applicable.
  • Keep the -e after soft c/g: noticeable, manageable, changeable.

✅ -ible

  • The root is not a complete word (often Latin): visible, audible, incredible, terrible, possible, feasible.
  • Root ends in a soft c or g (/s/, /dʒ/): eligible, legible, forcible.
  • Often after -ss or -ns: accessible, permissible, responsible, sensible.

🔄 Negatives & nouns

  • un- + -able: unacceptable, unaffordable, unbelievable.
  • in- / im- / il- / ir- + -ible: inaccessible, impossible, illegible, irresponsible.
  • Nouns: -able → -ability (reliability), -ible → -ibility (visibility, responsibility).
  • Adverbs: -able → -ably, -ible → -ibly (incredibly).

Other common suffixes

Word classSuffixExamples (towns & places)
Nouns-mentdevelop → development, govern → government, improve → improvement
-tion / -sion / -ationpollute → pollution, regenerate → regeneration, decide → decision
-ness / -ityfriendly → friendliness, safe → safety, popular → popularity, secure → security
-ance / -encemaintain → maintenance, reside → residence, exist → existence
-hood / -ship / -domneighbour → neighbourhood, citizen → citizenship, free → freedom
-er / -or / -ist / -ant / -ent (people)commute → commuter, visit → visitor, environment → environmentalist, inhabit → inhabitant, reside → resident
Adjectives-ful / -lesspeace → peaceful, harm → harmful / harmless, home → homeless
-ous / -al / -ic / -ydanger → dangerous, residence → residential, history → historic, noise → noisy
-ive / -ent / -antattract → attractive, depend → dependent, please → pleasant
Verbs-ise (-ize) / -en / -ifypedestrian → pedestrianise, modern → modernise, wide → widen, short → shorten, simple → simplify
Adverbs-lygradual → gradually, hope → hopefully, incredible → incredibly

🎯 Exam hack for the word-formation task

  • a / an / the / this + ___ + noun → adjective (a residential area).
  • the / his / a lot of + ___ → noun (the development). Check: singular or plural? people → residents, environmentalists.
  • to / will / can + ___ → verb (to widen).
  • ___ + verb / adjective or at the start of a sentence followed by a comma → adverb (Hopefully, …).
  • Does the sentence need a negative? Read the whole sentence: still inaccessible, totally unacceptable.

Maturita questions: Towns and places

🏠 My town

  • Describe the place where you live. What do you like and dislike about it?
  • What is there for young people to do?
  • Which three things would you like to have within a ten-minute walk of your home?

🏙️ Town vs countryside

  • Compare living in a city, a small town and a village.
  • Where would you like to live in ten years' time? Why?
  • Why do so many young people leave small towns?

⚠️ Problems & solutions

  • What are the biggest problems of your town?
  • If you were the mayor, what would you change first?
  • How can a neglected area be regenerated?

🔮 The town of the future

  • How will your town have changed by 2050?
  • Will smart cities make life better or just more controlled?
  • What will people be doing in today's shopping centres in 30 years?

🗺️ Describing a place

  • It's situated / located in the south of …
  • It's well known for … · It's a ten-minute walk from …
  • What I love most about it is …
  • The only drawback is that …

📈 Talking about change

  • Over the last few years, … has changed a lot.
  • … used to be …, but now it's …
  • … has been converted into … / renovated
  • In the near future, … is going to / is due to …

💬 Giving opinions

  • In my view, … should be a priority.
  • I'm not convinced that … · What we really need is …
  • It's bound to … unless …
  • It would be a big plus if …
📝 Model answer: "Describe your town and how it will change"

I live in Levice, a town of about 30,000 people in the south-west of Slovakia. It's well known for its castle and the picturesque park around it. What I love most is that everything is within walking distance: school, the indoor swimming pool and the main square. The only drawback is that young people have few places to meet in the evening, and some parts of town are quite run-down. Fortunately, things are changing. The council is going to renovate the old brewery, and I've heard it might be converted into a cultural centre. The work starts next spring, so by the time I finish university, it will have been completed. I think the town will become more attractive for young families, as long as there are enough affordable flats. In my view, a cycle lane to the station should be a priority – it's bound to reduce traffic congestion.

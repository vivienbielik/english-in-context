💡 Watch out: easily confused words

  • travel (uncountable verb/noun: I love travel) · a journey (A → B) · a trip (there and back, short) · a voyage (by sea) · a tour (several places, or around one place).
  • get in / out of a car or taxi (small, you bend down) · get on / off a bus, train, plane, bike (you can stand up inside or sit on top).
  • by car / by train but in my car / on the 7.15 train / on foot.
  • ride a bike, a horse · drive a car, a bus · fly a plane (= be the pilot) · sail a boat.
  • a ring road = obchvat, a roundabout = kruhový objazd. They are not the same thing.
  • a puncture = a hole in a tyre; a blowout = a tyre that bursts suddenly; a flat tyre = the result, the tyre has no air.

🌍 Travelling & Tourism Lab

Unit 17 turned into self-study tasks. Prepare your answers out loud or in writing; the model answers are hidden until you need them.

1 · Types of holidays

TASK 1: Speak about your holiday preferences

  • How far in advance do you like to plan your holidays? Do you book first minute or last minute?
  • What are the main criteria according to which you choose your holiday (price, distance, company, activities, accommodation)?

TASK 2: Talk about different types of holidays

  • Name the types of holidays below and compare their pluses and minuses.
  • Explain which age groups favour them and why.

📦 Package holiday

+ everything is organised (flights, hotel, transfers, meals); often cheap first/last-minute deals; feels safe

− little freedom; crowded resorts; you stay in a 'tourist bubble'

👥 Favoured by: families with small children, older people

🎒 Backpacking

+ cheap; total freedom; you meet people from all over the world

− tiring; little comfort; you must solve every problem yourself

👥 Favoured by: students, young adults on a gap year

🏛️ Sightseeing / city break

+ culture, history and architecture; short and easy to plan

− expensive cities; queues and crowds; lots of walking

👥 Favoured by: school trips, couples, culture lovers of all ages

🚐 Caravan holiday

+ your 'home' travels with you; flexible; cheaper than hotels

− slow on the road; campsite fees; cramped space; hard to park

👥 Favoured by: families, retired couples

🧘 Well-being holiday

+ rest, health, spa treatments, yoga; reduces stress

− can be expensive; may be boring for active people

👥 Favoured by: busy professionals, middle-aged and older people

🛣️ Road trip

+ flexibility; you discover hidden places off the main routes

− fuel, tolls and vignettes; long hours behind the wheel; traffic jams

👥 Favoured by: young adults, groups of friends

🚢 Cruise

+ many destinations without repacking; all-inclusive comfort

− pollution and overtourism in port cities; seasickness; only a few hours in each place

👥 Favoured by: retired people, older couples

⛺ Camping

+ close to nature; cheap; adventure

− weather, insects, little comfort and privacy

👥 Favoured by: teenagers, scouts, outdoor-loving families

🗣️ Useful language for comparing

Whereas a package holiday is …, backpacking is … · Unlike campers, people on a cruise … · The main drawback / advantage of … is that … · It appeals mostly to … because … · If I had to choose, I'd go for … · I'd never go on a cruise unless …

📝 Model answer (Task 2)

Package holidays and backpacking are almost opposites. If you book a package holiday, everything is arranged for you, which is why families with small children and older people love them. Backpackers, on the other hand, travel on a shoestring and sleep in hostels; the freedom is fantastic, provided you don't mind a bit of discomfort. Personally, I'm not a fan of cruises. They look luxurious, but they cause overtourism in port cities and I wish they weren't so popular. If I had more money, I'd go on a road trip around Scandinavia. Last year we went camping and it rained every day – I wish we had taken a caravan instead!

2 · Travelling in the future

TASK: Anticipate means of travelling in the future

  • Have you seen any time-travel films? Which ones?
  • Do you think people will ever achieve time travel? Why or why not?
  • Which factors could make travelling easier in the future?
  • Do you believe people will travel more in the future?
  • Do you reckon the hyperloop is a pipe dream or reality?

🔤 Key words

  • hyperloop – vysokorýchlostná doprava v nízkotlakovej trubici
  • maglev train – vlak na magnetickom vankúši
  • self-driving / autonomous car – samoriadiace auto
  • flying car, drone taxi – lietajúce auto, dronové taxi
  • space tourism – vesmírny turizmus
  • a pipe dream – nesplniteľný sen

🔮 Speculating

  • It's highly likely / unlikely that …
  • … will probably / might well / is bound to …
  • If engineers managed to …, we would …
  • Were the hyperloop to become reality, …
  • I wish we could already …
📝 Model answer

I don't think time travel will ever be possible, but transport will definitely change. Self-driving cars are already being tested, and if they were safe enough, there would be far fewer road accidents caused by drink-driving or speeding. Were the hyperloop to become reality, you could get from Bratislava to Košice in under an hour. At the moment it still sounds like a pipe dream – the infrastructure would be extremely expensive. Honestly, I wish we could just fix the trains we have: if they ran on time, I'd never take the car.

3 · Simulation: stranded on a desert island

TASK: Role-play

Imagine you are stranded on a desert island after a plane crash. Before jumping out of the plane, you had taken only three items with you. Demonstrate how you would use them to carry on your everyday activities. There is no electricity and no phone signal on the island.

🎯 Use the target grammar

  • Regret (past): I wish I had grabbed a knife instead of my phone.
  • Past → present consequence: If I hadn't taken the sunscreen, I would be badly burnt now.
  • Present wish: If only there were a signal here! I wish I could call my parents.
  • Hypothetical future: If a ship passed, I would use the mirror to signal.
  • Inversion: Had I known we would crash, I would have packed matches.

💭 Grammar Focus: Wish Clauses

After wish and if only, English moves the verb one step back in time. That step back does not mean the past; it means unreal: the opposite of reality.

Time we are talking aboutStructureModel sentenceReality
Present statewish + past simpleI wish I had a driving licence.I don't have one.
Present, in progresswish + past continuousI wish we weren't sitting in this jam.We are sitting in it.
Present / future abilitywish + couldI wish I could afford a flight to Japan.I can't.
Pastwish + past perfectI wish I had booked a window seat.I didn't.
Past abilitywish + could have + ppI wish I could have gone on the trip.I couldn't.
Change in others / future / annoyancewish + would + inf.I wish the bus would come!It isn't coming.

1 · Wish + past simple: an unreal present

wish + subject + past simple (were for all persons)
  • We are not happy with a present situation and would like it to be different.
  • were is used for all persons in formal and exam English: I wish I were …, I wish it were … was is common in informal speech.
  • Habits too: I wish I didn't live so far from school. (I commute an hour every day.)

Model sentences

  • I wish the trains in Slovakia were faster.
  • I wish our town had a proper ring road.
  • I wish I didn't get carsick on mountain roads.

2 · Wish + past continuous: an action happening now

wish + subject + was / were + -ing
  • We regret something that is in progress at this moment, or an arrangement.

Model sentences

  • I wish we weren't standing in this endless queue at security.
  • I wish it wasn't raining – we wanted to go hiking.
  • I wish I were lying on a beach instead of taking this test. / I wish I were going on the school trip next week.

3 · Wish + could: ability or possibility

wish + subject + could + infinitive
  • Present or future ability / possibility: I wish I could drive. Never I wish I can drive.
  • Compare: I wish I could drive (I don't know how) vs. I wish I drove to school (I know how, but I take the bus).
  • Use could instead of the impossible I wish I would: I wish I could stop worrying about the flight.

Model sentences

  • I wish I could speak Croatian – the ferry announcements are confusing.
  • I wish we could go to Iceland next summer, but it's too expensive.

4 · Wish + past perfect: regret about the past

wish + subject + had (not) + past participle · could have + past participle
  • Something happened (or didn't happen) in the past and we are sorry about it. It cannot be changed.
  • Negative regrets: I wish I hadn't eaten so much on the ferry.
  • Past ability: I wish I could have come with you to Rome. (= I wasn't able to.)
  • ❌ Never would have after wish in written / exam English: I wish I would have booked → I wish I had booked.

Model sentences

  • I wish I had checked the departure board – I went to the wrong gate.
  • I wish we hadn't taken the motorway; we got stuck in a tailback.
  • If only I had bought travel insurance!

5 · Wish + would: change, the future and annoyance

wish + another subject + would (not) + infinitive
  • Annoyance / criticism of someone's behaviour: I wish you wouldn't drive so fast!
  • A polite but firm request: I wish you would fasten your seatbelt.
  • A wish for a future change we can't control, often impatient: I wish it would stop snowing so the planes could take off.
  • Rule 1 – not with the same subject: I wish I would … → I wish I could … / I wish I did …
  • Rule 2 – not with present states: I wish the hotel would be closer to the beach. → I wish the hotel were closer. (Exception: be for behaviour that can change: I wish you would be quiet for a moment!)
  • Past simple or would? I wish he didn't smoke (a general fact about him) vs. I wish he wouldn't smoke in the car (I want him to stop this behaviour).

Model sentences

  • I wish the passengers would stop pushing!
  • I wish my dad wouldn't always leave packing until the last minute.
  • I wish the airline would answer my complaint.

6 · If only: a stronger wish

If only + the same tenses as wish
  • Same grammar as wish, but more emotional. It often ends with an exclamation mark.
  • If only I had a car! · If only the flight weren't delayed! · If only I hadn't missed the ferry! · If only they would open another check-in desk!
  • It can stand alone: "Could you come with us?" – "If only!" (= I'd love to, but I can't.)
  • Exception: if only + present / will expresses a real hope: If only the bus is on time, we'll make it.

7 · Other meanings of wish

  • wish + to-infinitive = formal want: I wish to complain about the delay. Passengers who wish to upgrade should go to desk 4.
  • wish + person + noun = a greeting: We wish you a pleasant flight. I wish you a safe journey.
  • wish for + noun: We wished for good weather all week.
  • wish vs. hope: hope is for real possibilities, followed by present / will: I hope the train is on time. wish is for unreal situations: I wish the train were on time (but it never is). You can't say I hope you a nice trip.

8 · Short answers with wish

Repeat only the auxiliary, one step back
QuestionShort answer

9 · When wish itself is in the past

  • The tense after wish does not change when wish becomes wished. It shows the time relative to the moment of wishing.
  • Same time: On the overnight bus, she wished she were at home. (She wasn't at home at that moment.)
  • Earlier: When the ferry left without us, we wished we had set off earlier.

10 · Related "unreal past" structures

  • would rather + another subject + past simple (present/future): I'd rather you didn't drive tonight. + past perfect (past): I'd rather you had told me the flight was cancelled. Same subject → infinitive: I'd rather take the train.
  • It's (high / about) time + subject + past simple: It's high time they repaired the potholes. Compare: It's time to go to the gate.
  • as if / as though: He drives as if he were in a Formula 1 race.
  • suppose / supposing: Supposing you missed your connection, what would you do?

⚡ Exceptions & common mistakes

  • I wish I would have more time. → I wish I had more time. (no would with the same subject)
  • I wish I would have known. / I wish I'd have known. → I wish I had known.
  • I wish I can come. → I wish I could come.
  • I wish the weather would be warmer today. → I wish the weather were warmer today. (state now) but ✓ I wish it would stop raining. (action/change)
  • I wish you have a nice holiday. → I hope you have a nice holiday. / I wish you a nice holiday.
  • I wish I hadn't to go. → I wish I didn't have to go. (present obligation) / I wish I hadn't had to go. (past)
  • If only I had more money, I would have bought … is fine only if it is mixed (present cause → past result). Check the time of each half!
  • was / were: I wish I was is acceptable in speech, but were is safer in the written exam, and obligatory in If I were you.

🇸🇰 Tip for Slovak speakers

  • Slovak uses the conditional: Kiežby som mal auto. / Škoda, že som si to nezarezervoval. English does not use would here; it moves the tense back: I wish I had a car. I wish I had booked it.
  • Kiežby…, Škoda, že…, Ľutujem, že…, Keby len… are all signals for wish / if only.
  • Želám ti šťastnú cestu = I wish you a safe journey – here wish is just a greeting.

⏳ Focus: Past Regret with a Consequence in the Present

A mistake made in the past often causes a problem now. We express it in three steps: the facts, the regret (wish + past perfect), and the imagined present (mixed conditional).

① RealityI forgot to renew my passport, so I can't fly to London tomorrow.
② RegretI wish I had renewed my passport.
③ Imagined presentIf I had renewed it, I could fly to London tomorrow.

Mixed conditional 3 → 2: past cause, present result

If + past perfect, would / could / might + infinitive (now, today, tomorrow, still)
  • We didn't reserve seats → we are standing now.
    I wish we had reserved seats. If we had reserved seats, we wouldn't be standing in the corridor now.
  • I didn't fill up the tank → we're stranded.
    If only I had filled up! If I had filled up, we wouldn't be stuck on the motorway now.
  • I didn't learn to drive → I depend on buses.
    I wish I had got my driving licence at 18. I could drive to work now.
  • The airline lost my luggage → I'm wearing borrowed clothes.
    If they hadn't lost my suitcase, I would be wearing my own clothes today.

Mixed conditional 2 → 3: present cause, past result

If + past simple (a permanent fact), would have + past participle
  • I am afraid of flying → I didn't go to Bali last summer.
    I wish I weren't so afraid of flying. If I weren't, I would have gone to Bali with my friends.
  • I don't speak Italian → I got lost in Rome.
    If I spoke Italian, I wouldn't have got lost.

🧭 How to choose the right form

  • Look for time markers in the result clause: now, today, at the moment, still, tomorrow, this week → would + infinitive / would be + -ing.
  • yesterday, last year, then, in 2020 → would have + past participle.
  • Is the cause a single past event (→ past perfect) or a permanent situation (→ past simple)?
  • ❌ If I had renewed my passport, I would have flown tomorrow. ✓ … I would fly / could fly tomorrow.
  • Inversion works too: Had I renewed my passport, I would be in London now.

🔀 Grammar Focus: Conditionals in the Context of Transport

A quick overview. For the full lesson, see Conditionals — Zero to Mixed.

TypeIf-clauseMain clauseModel sentence
Zero – facts, rulespresent simplepresent simpleIf you buy a ticket on the train, it costs more.
First – real futurepresent simplewill / can / imperativeIf the ferry is cancelled, we'll take the bus.
Second – unreal present / unlikely futurepast simplewould / could / might + inf.If public transport were free, I would never drive.
Third – unreal pastpast perfectwould have + ppIf we had left earlier, we wouldn't have missed the plane.
Mixed 3 → 2past perfectwould + inf. (now)If I had packed a charger, my phone would work now.
Mixed 2 → 3past simplewould have + ppIf I were more organised, I would have booked the hotel in time.

Alternatives to if

  • unless = if … not: You'll miss the train unless you hurry.
  • as long as / provided (that) / providing / on condition that = only if: You can borrow the car as long as you fill it up.
  • in case = as a precaution (≠ if): Take a jacket in case it gets cold on the plane.
  • otherwise / or (else): Book now, otherwise the tickets will sell out.
  • but for / without + noun: But for the GPS, we would have got lost.
  • suppose / supposing / imagine: Suppose the flight were cancelled – what would you do?
  • even if = it doesn't matter whether: I wouldn't go on a cruise even if it were free.

Inversion (formal and very useful at B2+)

  • Should + subject + inf. (first, unlikely): Should your luggage be lost, contact the airline desk.
  • Were + subject + to-inf / adjective (second): Were trains cheaper, more people would use them. Were the hyperloop to be built, …
  • Had + subject + pp (third / mixed): Had we checked the timetable, we wouldn't be waiting here now.
  • Negatives: Had I not overslept … (never Hadn't I overslept).

⚡ Exceptions you should know

  • No will / would in a normal if-clause: If it will rain, … → If it rains, …
  • will in the if-clause = willingness / insistence: If you will wait here, I'll get your boarding pass. If you will drive so fast, of course you get fined!
  • would in the if-clause = politeness: If you would follow me, please.
  • If + should = unlikely: If you should see my lost bag, please call me.
  • were to = very hypothetical: If the bridge were to collapse, the town would be cut off.
  • if vs. when: When we land, I'll call you (certain) · If we land on time, I'll call you (not sure).
  • Comma: use one when the if-clause comes first; usually none when it comes second.
  • in case never takes will: in case it will rain → in case it rains.

📝 Model answer: "Describe a journey that went wrong"

Last summer my family and I were supposed to fly from Vienna to Lisbon. We set off early, but there were roadworks on the motorway and we got stuck in a huge tailback. I wish we had taken the train – it would have been much more reliable. When we finally arrived, the check-in desk had already closed and we heard the final call for our flight. We had to buy new plane tickets for the next day and spend the night in an expensive airport hotel. On top of that, my suitcase was lost on the second flight. If the airline hadn't lost it, I wouldn't be so angry with them even now. Had we left home an hour earlier, none of this would have happened. Now I always plan my route and leave plenty of time, in case something goes wrong. If only transport in Europe were as punctual as the timetables promise!

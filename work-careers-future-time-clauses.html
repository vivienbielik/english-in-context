💡 Watch out: easily confused words

  • job (countable: a job) · work (uncountable: I'm looking for work, never a work) · career = your whole working life · profession = a job needing special training.
  • salary = paid monthly, usually to professionals · wages = paid weekly or per hour.
  • resign = you decide to leave · be fired / sacked = for doing something wrong · be made redundant / laid off = the company doesn't need you · retire = stop working because of age.
  • earn money (for work) · win money (in a lottery) · gain experience.
  • apply for a job · apply to a company / a university.
  • an employer gives work · an employee does it · the staff = all the employees (plural verb is common).
  • pension = money you receive when you are old. A small hotel is a guesthouse or a B&B, never a pension!

📰 Reading: Will a Robot Take Your Job?

Read the article about the future of work. It is full of the grammar from this lesson – you will analyse it in Exercise 7.

1"What do you want to be when you grow up?" is a question that teenagers hate. And no wonder: experts estimate that many of today's school students will be doing jobs that don't exist yet. By the time you finish university, artificial intelligence will have changed almost every profession, from accounting to journalism.

2That doesn't mean robots are about to take all our jobs. Looking back at history, we can see that new technology destroys some jobs but creates others. When cash machines appeared in the 1970s, people predicted that bank clerks would disappear. In fact, banks opened more branches, employing staff to sell loans and give advice instead of counting notes.

3Still, the change is happening faster than ever. A large European bank is going to close half of its call centres next year, and some companies are already taking on fewer graduates for routine office work. Having lost their jobs to automation, many older workers find retraining difficult, especially if they have never used digital tools.

4So which skills will employers be looking for in ten years? Surprisingly, most of them are soft skills: creativity, empathy, critical thinking and the ability to keep up with constant change. Machines are excellent at carrying out repetitive tasks, but they are not very good at comforting a patient or persuading a client. What a relief for nurses, teachers and salespeople!

5Career advisers therefore recommend that students stop worrying about the "perfect" job. Instead of following a fixed career path, you will probably change jobs several times before you retire. As soon as you leave school, start collecting experience: internships, volunteering and part-time jobs all count. By the time you are thirty, you will have built a mix of skills that no robot can copy.

🔮 Future Forms: All the Ways to Talk About the Future

English has no single future tense. The form you choose shows how sure you are and when you decided.

FormWe use it for…Example
will + infinitivepredictions based on opinion (I think, probably), instant decisions, promises, offers, facts about the futureI think AI will change every job. · I'll send you my CV tonight.
be going to + infinitiveplans and intentions decided before; predictions based on what we can see nowI'm going to apply for a summer job. · Look at the queue – we're going to be late.
present continuousfixed arrangements with a time and place (diary)I'm having a job interview on Monday at 9.
present simpletimetables, schedules, programmesThe training course starts on 3 March.
future continuous
will be + -ing		an action in progress at a certain time in the future; polite questions about plansThis time next year I'll be working in London. · Will you be using the printer?
future perfect simple
will have + 3rd form		an action finished before a point in the future (by, by the time)By 2030, I will have finished my studies.
future perfect continuous
will have been + -ing		how long an action will have lasted at a point in the futureIn June, she will have been working here for 20 years.
be about to / be on the point of + -ing / be on the verge ofthe very near futureThe company is about to lay off 200 workers.
be due to · be to (formal)official plans, expected timesThe new manager is due to start in May. · The President is to visit the factory.

🔮 will or going to?

decision NOW → will · decision BEFORE → going to
A: The printer isn't working. B: Don't worry, I'll fix it. (decided now)
I've saved some money. I'm going to start my own business. (decided before)
The sky is black – it's going to rain. (evidence) · I think it will rain tomorrow. (opinion)

📅 going to or present continuous?

intention → going to · arranged with other people (time, place) → present continuous
I'm going to look for a new job. (my plan) · I'm meeting the HR manager at 10 tomorrow. (arranged)

⏩ Future continuous vs future perfect

8:00 start work→10:00 ✱ I'll be working→16:00 finish→18:00 ✱ I'll have finished
At 10 o'clock tomorrow I'll be working. (in progress) · By 6 o'clock I'll have finished. (completed)

⏪ The future in the past

was / were going to · would · was about to · was due to
I was going to apply, but I missed the deadline. (a plan that didn't happen)
She knew she would get the job. · We were about to leave when the phone rang.

⚠️ Typical mistakes

  • When I will finish school, I will travel. → When I finish school, I will travel. (present after when – see Time Clauses)
  • I will have an interview tomorrow at 9. → I'm having an interview tomorrow at 9. (an arrangement)
  • Wait, I'm going to help you! → Wait, I'll help you! (an instant offer)
  • By 2030 I will finish my studies. → By 2030 I will have finished my studies.
  • Shall you come to the meeting? → Will you come…? (shall only with I / we in offers and suggestions: Shall I help?)

⏱️ Time Clauses

Sentences with when, as soon as, until, before, after, by the time… follow one simple rule that students break all the time.

⏱️ The golden rule

time conjunction + PRESENT, main clause + FUTURE
When I finish school, I will look for a job. ✅ (not when I will finish ❌)
I'll call you as soon as I get the results.
ConjunctionMeaningExample
whenat the time thatWhen the contract ends, I'll travel.
as soon as / the moment / onceimmediately afterOnce you sign the contract, you can start.
before / afterearlier than / later thanRead the job ad carefully before you apply.
until / tillup to the time whenI'll stay at the shop until the manager comes.
while / asduring the time thatI'll answer emails while you're at lunch.
by the timebefore or at the time whenBy the time you get here, I'll have left.
wheneverevery time thatCall me whenever you need help.

✅ Present perfect = the first action will be COMPLETED

when / after / as soon as / once / until + present perfect
I'll go home when I've finished the report. (first finish, then go)
You can't leave until you've signed the contract.

🔁 Time clauses about the past

past simple / past perfect · future in the past (would)
After she had graduated, she moved to Bratislava.
He promised he would call me as soon as he arrived.

⚠️ Don't overdo it: when can be followed by will

  • If when starts a question or an indirect question, it's not a time clause: When will you know? · I don't know when they will call. ✅
  • With until the verb is positive, never negative: Wait until I come. ✅ Wait until I don't come. ❌
  • by the time + present → main clause often in the future perfect: By the time I retire, I'll have worked for 40 years.

😮 Exclamations and Interjections

Show your feelings in English: surprise, admiration, relief or disappointment. Very useful in the speaking exam and in informal letters.

What a / an …!

  • What a (+ adjective) + singular countable noun
  • What a great job!
  • What an interesting offer!

What …! (no article)

  • What (+ adjective) + uncountable or plural noun
  • What awful weather! · What good news!
  • What lovely colleagues!

How …!

  • How + adjective / adverb
  • How exciting! · How quickly she learns!
  • How + clause: How I hate Mondays!

such / so

  • such a + adj + noun: It's such a stressful job!
  • such + adj + plural / uncountable: such long hours!
  • so + adj / adv: She's so talented!

Negative questions

  • Used as exclamations, with falling intonation
  • Isn't it wonderful!
  • Wasn't that interview awful!

⚠️ Watch out

  • What a good news! → What good news! (news, advice, information, work, weather are uncountable)
  • How a nice office! → What a nice office! / How nice the office is!
  • It's so interesting job. → It's such an interesting job.
  • Word order after How / What: subject before verb – How fast he types! (not How fast does he type!)

🔧 Phrasal Verbs for the World of Work

You learned the meanings on the cards. Now learn the grammar: where does the object go?

Separable

  • The object can go before or after the particle
  • turn down the offer / turn the offer down
  • A pronoun must go in the middle: turn it down ✅ turn down it ❌
  • take on, lay off, set up, fill in, carry out, put off, turn down, take over, work out, hand in

Inseparable

  • The object always goes after the particle
  • apply for it · look into the problem
  • apply for, look into, deal with, look after

No object (intransitive)

  • get ahead, step down, burn out, drop out, break down
  • She burned out after two years.

Three-part verbs

  • Never separate them
  • keep up with, stand in for, drop out of, come up with, look forward to (+ -ing!)
  • I look forward to hearing from you.

✂️ Shortening Sentences with -ing

Participle clauses make your writing sound advanced. The key rule: both parts must have the same subject.

✂️ What is a participle clause?

two clauses with the SAME subject → replace one with -ing
When she walked into the office, she saw her new boss. → Walking into the office, she saw her new boss.
It makes your writing shorter and more formal – perfect for the written Maturita.
MeaningLong versionShort version
at the same timeWhile I was waiting for the interview, I read my notes.Waiting for the interview, I read my notes.
reason / causeBecause he didn't have a degree, he couldn't apply.Not having a degree, he couldn't apply.
one action after another (perfect participle)After she had finished school, she found a job.Having finished school, she found a job.
passiveBecause he had been fired twice, he was nervous.Having been fired twice, he was nervous.
after a conjunctionBefore you sign it, read the contract.Before signing it, read the contract.
resultPrices rose, and this made life harder.Prices rose, making life harder.

🔗 Conjunctions you can keep

after · before · while · when · since · on (= as soon as) · by (= how) · instead of · without + -ING
Since leaving school, he has had three jobs. · On hearing the news, she phoned her mum.
You can get ahead by learning languages. · He left without saying goodbye.

⚠️ The dangling participle – the classic mistake

  • Walking into the office, the phone rang. → the phone wasn't walking! → Walking into the office, I heard the phone ring.
  • Having finished the report, the boss praised me. → Having finished the report, I was praised by the boss.
  • Negative: not goes before the -ing: Not knowing the answer, … (not Knowing not…)

Three big career decisions

Work in pairs or small groups. Choose one decision, add your own arguments and reach a conclusion together.

🎓 University or apprenticeship?

+ University: higher salary later, more career options

− University: years without income, no guarantee of a job

+ Apprenticeship: you earn while you learn, practical skills

− Apprenticeship: fewer chances of promotion to management

💶 Money or job satisfaction?

+ Well-paid job: security, you can afford a home and travel

− Well-paid job: long hours, stress, risk of burnout

+ Dream job: motivation, you look forward to Mondays

− Dream job: often badly paid, hard to make ends meet

🏢 Employee or self-employed?

+ Employee: regular salary, paid holiday, sick pay

− Employee: a boss, a fixed schedule

+ Self-employed: freedom, you choose your projects

− Self-employed: irregular income, no paid holiday

🗨️ Useful phrases

  • Talking about plans: I'm planning to … · I'm hoping to … · By the time I'm 30, I'll have … · This time next year I'll be …
  • Agreeing / disagreeing: That's a fair point, but … · I see what you mean, however … · I couldn't agree more.
  • Reacting: What a great idea! · How interesting! · Wow, I'd never thought of that!
